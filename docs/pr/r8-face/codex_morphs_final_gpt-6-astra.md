The top five fixes, in priority order:

1. **Tile 5 — rebuild the squeezed-eye lid meeting line.**  
   **Evidence:** Front has an approximate “><” symbol, but 3/4 and especially side expose an open wedge with a long lower black stroke. **Fix:** Define a shared closed-lid seam on the curved eye surface, with two short segments meeting at the squeeze point. Bring the upper and lower lid margins onto that seam and drive their lash roots from the corresponding lid vertices. Taper the lower stroke before the outer corner instead of letting it extend across the cheek-facing surface. The upper and lower margins should meet without crossing.

2. **Tile 4 — remove the outer-corner lash stretching.**  
   **Evidence:** The smiling arc is readable, but 3/4 and side show thin protruding lash tips and overlapping-looking edges at the outer corner. **Fix:** Reduce the displacement gradient over the outermost lid vertices and carry each lash base with the local lid surface orientation. Preserve lash length and thickness during the morph, and add a corrective shape for the outer lash junction so it forms one clean tapered silhouette. Check 1 and 2 afterward because their closed-eye corners show a milder version of the same pinching.

3. **Tile 30 — give the tongue an unmistakable free tip.**  
   **Evidence:** Front and 3/4 show a rounded pink patch with internal creases, without a clear separation between tongue, mouth cavity, and lower lip. **Fix:** Keep the tongue root inside the oral cavity, bring only the rounded tip forward over the lower lip, and adjust the lower lip to provide clearance. Give the tip enough thickness and a shallow downward bend to create a distinct silhouette; recess the cavity behind it so the tongue does not visually merge with the mouth fill. A centered side-mouth render is needed to check the final clearance.

4. **Tile 25 — reshape the opening into a triangle.**  
   **Evidence:** Front and 3/4 show a peaked upper edge over an otherwise rounded oval. **Fix:** Move the upper central lip vertices into a single apex, spread the two lower corners, and flatten the lower boundary. Distribute the intervening lip vertices along two sloping sides with lightly rounded corners. Move the inner mouth rim with the outer rim so the triangular contour does not expose a mismatched oval interior.

5. **Tile 21 — soften the wide-open mouth’s boxy corners.**  
   **Evidence:** Front shows a broad opening with abrupt transitions from the upper edge into nearly vertical sides; 3/4 preserves that blocky impression. **Fix:** Redistribute the lip-ring vertices into a broad, rounded opening, smoothing the upper corners and lower arc. Spread the opening deformation into the surrounding lower-face vertices rather than concentrating it at the rim, preserving a continuous lip thickness as the mouth expands.
