The **top five concrete problems**, ordered by visual impact, are:

1. **Tile 5 — squeezed-eye contour becomes a long wedge in 3/4 and side.**  
   The straight lower segment and heavy upper lash branch do not merge into a convincing compact squeeze. **Fix:** place a shared squeeze point near the inner portion of each eye and form two short branches opening toward the outer corner. Keep both branches close to the facial surface, taper their outer ends, and merge or overlap their inner endpoints cleanly. Move the existing lash vertices with the upper branch so the lash mass does not form an extra spur. Check the silhouette in all three views.

2. **Tile 35 — tears have an overly sideways, ribbon-like silhouette in 3/4 and side.**  
   The oblique views expose the streak’s flattened shape much more than the front view. The images suggest an orientation or surface-following problem, but do not prove that the geometry is detached. **Fix:** reshape each tear as a narrow strip whose centerline travels downward from the lower lid along the cheek. Conform its vertices to the cheek with a small, consistent surface offset, taper its width, and give the terminal drop a rounded volume. Avoid extending the strip laterally away from the eye.

3. **Tile 4 — smiling closed eyes retain the curvature of a relaxed blink.**  
   Front and 3/4 show the middle of the closed-eye line sitting lower than the ends, weakening the smile. **Fix:** raise the central portion of the meeting lid contours into a shallow upward arch, with the corners lower than the middle. Bring the lower lid upward to meet the upper lid along that same curve and carry the lashes with it. A small cheek lift can support the expression, but the lid curvature is the primary correction.

4. **Tile 25 — the triangular mouth lacks a triangular aperture.**  
   Both front and 3/4 read as another rounded open mouth; a light interior patch does not supply the missing silhouette. **Fix:** reshape the actual lip opening into a rounded triangle: one upper apex, two lower lateral corners, and a comparatively flat lower edge. Redistribute the surrounding lip vertices smoothly to avoid pinching at the apex. Keep the teeth behind the aperture so their exposed edge does not obscure its shape.

5. **Tile 23 — the inverted-V frown has secondary bends at the corners.**  
   Front and 3/4 show a central arch plus small hooked ends, which makes the mouth look wavy. **Fix:** move the central lip-seam vertices upward and both corners downward, then interpolate each half as one smooth, monotonic slope. Remove the local direction reversals near the corners. Keep the upper and lower lip boundaries together throughout the closed seam.
