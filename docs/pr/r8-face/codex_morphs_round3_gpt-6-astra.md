The five highest-priority fixes are:

1. **Tile 5: rebuild the squeezed-eye corner as a coherent lid-and-lash junction.**  
   **Evidence:** In **3/4 and side**, the lower diagonal extends into a long isolated stroke while the upper strokes bunch into a sharp outer-corner cluster. In front, the same construction is less conspicuous.  
   **Geometric fix:** Give the upper and lower lid contours a shared, stable corner target, then shape the two arms of the squeeze around it. Shorten the lower lash strip and taper its width toward the endpoint. Drive lash roots from the corresponding lid positions, and inspect the intermediate weights so the two strips meet without crossing or producing a fold.

2. **Tile 4: remove the pinched outer-corner extension.**  
   **Evidence:** In **3/4 and side**, the smiling lid has an irregular overlapping tip and a thin skin-colored sliver extending beyond the main dark curve. The sheet cannot distinguish with certainty whether that sliver is a folded lid surface or an exposed adjoining strip.  
   **Geometric fix:** Reduce the outermost lid vertices’ displacement and distribute the smiling arch over more of the lid. Keep the corner anchored to the face, smooth the adjacent skin transition, and recalculate the lash strip from that corrected curve with consistent surface clearance. Check the corner faces for inversion before simply hiding the sliver.

3. **Tile 9: give determination a distinct eye aperture.**  
   **Evidence:** In **front and 3/4**, it remains too close to the calm and flat-stare states.  
   **Geometric fix:** Lower the inner portion of each upper lid relative to its outer portion to create a deliberate slant, rather than reducing the whole aperture almost uniformly. Add a small coordinated lower-lid lift if needed, keeping the lash roots attached and preserving clearance from the eyeball. Make this eye shape readable independently of the subtle brow morphs.

4. **Tile 25: define the triangular mouth at the lip boundary.**  
   **Evidence:** In **front and 3/4**, the aperture looks like a rounded open mouth with a pointed top, rather than a clear “▲.”  
   **Geometric fix:** Set a centered upper apex, two lower lateral corners, and a flatter lower edge. Distribute boundary vertices along those three edges with slight rounding at the corners to avoid pinching. Match the inner mouth rim to the new aperture, and keep the upper teeth recessed enough that their pale edge does not visually cut across the apex.

5. **Tile 35: connect the tears to the lower-lid region.**  
   **Evidence:** In **front**, both drops appear suspended below the eyes; **3/4 and side** make the isolated near-cheek drop especially apparent.  
   **Geometric fix:** Extend a narrow tear neck or shallow wet ribbon from the lower-lid margin to each drop. Fit it to the cheek with a small positive surface offset and taper its width into the drop. Preserve that clearance through eyelid morphs so the attachment neither floats away nor sinks into the cheek.

I would not rate any tile **broken** from these sheets alone: the visible issues are localized shape and readability problems, and the supplied views do not show a confirmed major mesh tear or unmistakable tooth/tongue penetration.
