# Codex review: gpt-6.1-sol

The body is generally coherent in the rest views, with rounded limb volumes and no obvious open seams, but the squat exposes a significant knee deformation problem: the thigh-to-shin transition bends into a continuous rubbery hook rather than retaining a distinct knee. Fix that deformation first, then refine the shoulder-to-armpit transition visible with the arms overhead. The existing body code already provides closely spaced joint loops, so the next pass should concentrate on cross-section shape and the distribution of helper-bone weights. Hand volume and the exposed leg silhouette need smaller refinements after those changes. These scores are body-focused; clothing obscures most elbow and hip deformation, and the supplied Blender poses do not establish equivalent behavior after PMX export.

Scores: naturalness 7/10, animation 6/10, fidelity 7/10

## body

- **P1** [animation] *squat q34_front_left, squat front* - both knees, from the lower thigh around the front of the joint into the upper shin: The bent legs form broad, continuous hooks: the front of each knee has little distinct patellar structure, while the lower contour develops a flattened shelf before reaching the shin. This makes the legs read as flexible tubing under a routine squat, even though the surfaces remain connected. The existing dense LEG_ROWS make insufficient loop count an unlikely primary cause.
  - Fix: In the body's section of levi/rig.py, preserve the existing broad posterior JOINT_HINGE transition but give the anterior knee a more localized rotation distribution. Add a patella helper taking approximately half the knee rotation, weight a 2–3 cm anterior patch predominantly to it, and blend back into the existing thigh and shin helpers over another 3–4 cm. In body.py, keep that patch rounded through KNEECAP and leg_offset rather than enlarging the whole knee ring. Compare the resulting outer contour at 60, 90 and 120 degrees of flexion before changing KNEE_FWD.
- **P2** [naturalness] *clay q34_front_left, q34_front_left* - character's left hand, across the distal palm and the four finger roots: The palm reads as a smooth oval pad with nearly parallel cylindrical fingers attached to its end. The rounded palm-to-finger transition is weaker than the wrist-to-palm transition, making the hand look assembled from separate primitives in the clay view.
  - Fix: Refine _palm_shape(), the distal HAND_R entries and the FINGERS base positions in body.py together. Stagger the finger roots by approximately 0.2–0.4 cm along the hand, form shallow web valleys between them, and carry each metacarpal dome into its finger root. Retain the five separate finger islands required by fit_fingers(), but bury their proximal caps approximately 0.3 cm inside the palm and taper that overlap so it does not produce visible root collars.
- **P2** [animation] *arms_up q34_front_left* - character's left axilla and inner upper arm, immediately above the bodice neckline: The raised upper arm retains its outer volume, but the inner shoulder transition runs down into a narrow, pointed crease at the chest. It is a small deformation defect rather than a tear, yet it makes the shoulder look attached through a tapered strip instead of rolling into an axillary hollow.
  - Fix: Refine the inner quadrant of arm_rings() and its SOCKET_STEPS transition in body.py, spreading the turn across the existing transition rings and filling the pointed end by roughly 0.2–0.4 cm. In the body's _shoulder_stages weighting, introduce a smooth angular bias around the socket so the axillary vertices retain more chest/shrug influence while the outer cap follows the arm stages. Keep the partition continuous around the socket; do not move all SHOULDER_STAGES knots together or inflate the entire deltoid.
- **P2** [fidelity] *sheet side / render side, sheet front / render front* - exposed lower thighs immediately below the petticoat, continuing to the upper calf: The rendered legs narrow more strongly just below the hem and then expand into a pronounced calf bulge. The reference carries more fullness through the upper exposed leg, giving a gentler transition. This is particularly visible in the side comparison and contributes to the reduced rendered skin area there; correcting it should remain subordinate to the squat fix.
  - Fix: Adjust the exposed-leg profile around rows 620–670 in LEG_FRONT and LEG_SIDE, adding roughly 0.2–0.4 cm to the deficient anterior and lateral contours and easing the change toward the calf over 4–6 cm. Check the contribution from _leg_master() in that interval and reduce its local influence where it reinstates the constriction. Keep knee_joint(), ankle_joint() and the lower ankle profile fixed during this proportion pass so the rig and shoe fit do not drift.

They are minor at full-character scale, but they make the pale skin look chipped when the hands are inspected closely.   - Fix: Inspect the body hand's fill normals separately from its outline hull.
