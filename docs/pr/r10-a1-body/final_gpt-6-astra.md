# Codex review: gpt-6-astra

The body is broadly coherent in the standing views, but the squat exposes the main remaining weakness: the knee bends through an overly long, rounded section, making the knee-to-shin transition look rubbery. The raised-arm pose also shows some narrowing through the shoulder and armpit, although the supplied views do not show an open seam or a detached limb. Rest-pose improvements should concentrate on the knee-to-calf transition and the palm-to-finger construction rather than changing the overall proportions. The build already provides substantial joint-loop density and staged deformation helpers, so the next pass should refine local shape and weight distribution rather than add subdivision indiscriminately. These scores concern the body; the still images do not establish motion stability or equivalent deformation after PMX export.

Scores: naturalness 7/10, animation 6/10, fidelity 7/10

## body

- **P1** [animation] *squat q34_front_left; squat front* - Both knees, from the lower thigh across the kneecap into the upper shin: The bent knee reads as a rounded hose bend: the front projects into a broad lobe while the upper shin curves back sharply before reaching the ankle. This is conspicuous in the three-quarter squat and makes the leg appear to bend along its length rather than around a supported joint. The code already has closely spaced knee rings, so insufficient subdivision is not the main problem.
  - Fix: Keep body.py's existing LEG_ROWS and KNEE_FWD as the starting point. In rig.py, refine the knee's JOINT_HINGE partition so the anterior kneecap rings retain a more coherent volume and the distal upper-shin rings return to the shin bone sooner, while preserving the broader posterior blend needed for the knee hollow. Introduce a separate anterior/side falloff instead of narrowing the entire hinge band uniformly; start by shortening only the distal anterior transition by 1–2 cm. Compare 60, 90 and 120 degree bends from the side and three-quarter views, checking both the lobe and posterior face inversion before adopting the change.
- **P2** [naturalness] *q34_front_left; clay q34_front_left; render side* - Both legs, immediately above the knee and along the upper calf: The resting leg has a noticeable inward transition above a relatively abrupt calf swell. In three-quarter view the knee is less clearly articulated than the calf bulge, producing a mildly wavy outline that becomes more conspicuous when the leg bends.
  - Fix: In body.py, isolate the shape-master contribution around rows 650–735. Test reducing KNEE_MASTER_W from 1.0 to approximately 0.6–0.75 while retaining the low-order radial fit, then adjust LEG_SIDE and the local leg_offset contribution to move the upper-calf fullness slightly downward and fill the pre-knee hollow by about 0.2–0.3 cm. Blend that correction over at least 3 cm and preserve the anterior shin line; do not enlarge KNEE_WIDE globally.
- **P2** [naturalness] *q34_front_left; high; clay front* - Both hands, across the knuckle row and thumb-to-palm junction: The hands still read as broad, fairly flat palms with similarly aligned tubular fingers. The thumb junction is abrupt, and the knuckle row does not provide enough visible transition between the palm mass and the individual digits. This is a form issue in the supplied views; they do not demonstrate finger-animation failure.
  - Fix: Refine _palm_shape() and HAND_R locally rather than increasing the whole palm's thickness: add approximately 0.1–0.2 cm of dorsal arch beneath the middle and ring knuckles, fading toward the index and little-finger edges. Adjust the FINGERS base positions and first two rings to bury their roots more smoothly into that arch, and broaden the thenar-to-thumb transition by about 0.1 cm. Preserve the five separate finger islands required by rig.fit_fingers, and check that the root overlaps remain concealed during finger curl.
- **P2** [animation] *arms_up front; arms_up q34_front_left* - Both shoulder sockets, especially the inner upper arm and armpit beneath the sleeve attachment: Raising the arms leaves a narrow, stretched-looking skin connection beside the chest instead of a fuller shoulder flowing into a compact armpit fold. The surface remains connected, but the volume loss is visible compared with the relaxed shoulder.
  - Fix: Use the signed levi_socket_s parameter already generated by body.py to inspect the socket transition rings separately from the arm shaft. In rig.py, bias the inner socket toward the intermediate shoulder helpers rather than letting it reach full arm rotation as quickly as the outer cap; start with a 0.5–1 cm local extension of the inner transition. If the contour remains hollow, add only 0.2–0.3 cm of front/back fullness to the inner transition in arm_rings(), fading to zero at the socket border and first full arm ring. Recompute the socket attribute after the shape change and verify overhead and lowered arms.
- **P3** [fidelity] *render front; render side; render back* - Exposed legs, upper chest and arms: The body skin is consistently lighter and slightly pinker than the reference, particularly over the large leg surfaces. The supplied regional measurements support this difference, although the skin region also includes the head and should not be treated as a body-only measurement.
  - Fix: After the shape changes, make a small adjustment to the skin palette used by body.py: test lowering the base colour's lightness by roughly 2–3 Lab units and reducing its pink saturation slightly. Keep the current skin shade threshold initially, since the measured shade shares are already close to the sheet, and compare masked body areas under the same lighting before changing the shade colour.
