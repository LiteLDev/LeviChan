# Codex review: gpt-6.1-sol

The back hair has a continuous scalp-to-back mass, but its lower third still reads as overlapping sculpted wedges and heavy hooks rather than flowing locks. The most important fixes are to resolve the crowded lower tips, improve the outer locks' shape through depth, and remove the fragmented cel terminators visible across the pale mass. The clay views show that these problems involve geometry and section orientation as well as shading; changing the global ramp alone would leave the underlying forms unresolved. The supplied poses show no clear back-hair tearing or major collapse, although the swing exposes an awkward broad, angular transition in an outer lock. After those form and deformation fixes, refine the two lower lobes and their mint transitions to recover the reference's more deliberate strand flow.

Scores: naturalness 5/10, animation 7/10, fidelity 6/10

## hair_back

- **P1** [naturalness] *clay back, clay q34_back_right, q34_back_right* - Lower central mass, immediately above and between the three prominent bottom curls: Several tapering locks emerge as pointed wedges and abruptly disappear into neighboring surfaces. The clay view exposes crowded changes of depth and dark triangular pockets; the cel view turns the same area into a chipped, irregular fringe. This interrupts the downward flow of the main lobes and makes the hair look assembled from intersecting pieces.
  - Fix: In levi/parts/hair_back.py, separate the terminal paths of MAIN B/C/G, WAVES, and the lower SWEEP lock around rows 370–445. For ends intended to be buried, replace the common TUCK_PX=6 target with a per-lock burial depth that puts the entire terminal section 0.5–1.0 cm beneath its covering lock, fading over the final 6–9 cm. For exposed ends, stagger their heights by 1–3 cm and preserve a distinct rounded taper rather than allowing a wedge to intersect another lock midway across its face.
- **P1** [naturalness] *q34_front_left, q34_back_right, clay q34_front_left, turn_045, turn_135* - Outer hanging locks behind both sleeves, from upper-arm height to the upward-pointing crescent tips: The long outer locks read as broad straps ending in sickle-shaped hooks. Their front/back silhouettes are recognizable, but the three-quarter views reveal long, comparatively uniform faces and a sharp change from the broad hanging section into the thin upright tip. They need a more convincing continuous turn through space.
  - Fix: Rework the wave and crescent entries in OUTER_LEFT rather than increasing thickness globally; these already use th=0.78 and 0.7. Replace the fixed normal hints with parallel-transported section frames, then apply the authored twist continuously along arc length. Add depth variation of roughly 1–2 cm across the lower S bend, distribute the roll over its last 8–12 cm, and narrow the broad run by about 10–15% where it currently reads as a strap. Preserve a rounded section through the hook's throat before tapering to the tip.
- **P1** [naturalness] *back, q34_back_right, side, islands back, islands q34_back_right* - Pale rear surface below the shoulder blades and along the lower overlapping locks: Thin, disconnected shade marks and angular terminator fragments make the pale hair look scratched. The supplied hair-region measurements show 13–15 small islands in each rear, side, and review view, and the visible concentration across the back mass confirms that this is more than an isolated tip artifact. Those counts include hair_front, so they should not all be attributed to this part.
  - Fix: In set_volume_normals(), make the shared normal field dominant on buried and nearly flush lock boundaries, and fade own-normal influence smoothly over several rings before a lock becomes exposed. Use a spatially continuous blend through the VOL_LOBES valley rather than allowing short sections to cross the toon threshold independently. For residual marks, compare casts on/off for hair_back_side and bury or separate the responsible overlap; retain its broad shadow onto the dress instead of disabling all back-hair casting.
- **P2** [naturalness] *side, clay side, clay q34_back_right* - Rear bulge from shoulder-blade height through the upper side curl: The side silhouette swells into a relatively solid bulb, with a short angular waist before the lower curls. In clay, the broad backing volume and the surface locks do not consistently describe the same flowing form, making the side mass feel heavier and more block-like than the rear silhouette suggests.
  - Fix: Resample _curtain_rows() longitudinally at approximately 8–12 panel-pixel intervals using the existing stable() interpolation, and raise CURTAIN_STEP from 13 to about 21 across the arc. Coordinate the backing profile with MASS_BACK and the SWEEP spines so the shoulder-to-curl transition has continuous curvature. Keep the backing recessed about 0.5–1.0 cm beneath the visible locks instead of allowing it to become the exposed side contour.
- **P2** [naturalness] *clay back, clay q34_back_right, back* - Bottom curls of MAIN A, D, and F: The bottom hooks have thick, rounded throats followed by narrow returning points, producing a repeated padded C shape. Their similar curl construction is especially apparent in clay and makes the lower edge look like a row of molded ornaments rather than varied hair bundles.
  - Fix: Give MAIN A/D/F separate width and thickness profiles through hook() instead of relying primarily on the shared CURL_TAPER and CURL_TH values. Reduce width and thickness through the final half of the two smaller curls by roughly 15–25%, vary their return angles by 10–20 degrees, and stagger their depth by 0.5–1.5 cm. Preserve one dominant open curl and make the neighboring returns subordinate, with clear gaps between their tips and adjacent locks.
- **P2** [animation] *swing front* - Image-left outer lock, between its shoulder-level departure and the hanging crescent: The swung lock exposes a broad angular transition near the shoulder. It remains connected, but the change in direction reads as a hinged ribbon rather than a distributed bend, which will become conspicuous during secondary motion.
  - Fix: Store each outer lock's spine parameter and stable lock identifier in clump(), and use them to assign its chain and longitudinal weights explicitly. In levi/rig.py, place an additional chain joint at the outer lock's principal shoulder-level direction change and blend across at least three adjacent mesh rings. Keep all vertices of a cross-section on the same longitudinal parameter so its inner and outer sides do not acquire different bend locations.
- **P2** [fidelity] *sheet back versus render back, sheet side versus render side* - Main rear mass from the shoulder blades to the curled ends: The reference separates into two long, flowing lower lobes, while the render retains a broad central curtain for longer and then breaks into numerous short points. This changes both the negative space and the visual weight of the hairstyle. The rear hair-region area is also larger in the render, consistent with the visibly fuller mass, although region classification is approximate.
  - Fix: After resolving the overlaps, deepen and broaden the geometric lobe separation gradually below row 300: use a trial VALLEY depth of 12–14 px instead of 8 px and a half-width of 26–30 px instead of 20 px. Reshape CURTAIN_HW's last rows into two recessed backing lobes so the shell does not fill the intended separation. Extend selected primary paths into the reference's longer S flow while shortening or burying secondary points that interrupt it.
- **P2** [fidelity] *sheet back versus render back, q34_back_right* - Lower central lock ends and their transition into the mint curls: Several exposed central ends remain almost white immediately above much greener hooks, so the color change emphasizes the stacked construction. The reference carries mint more coherently through the lower lobes. This is a local gradient-placement problem rather than evidence that the entire hair palette should be darkened.
  - Fix: In grad_global() and clump(), retain the shared root-to-lower-mass field but add a smooth terminal adjustment for exposed primary ends. Blend that adjustment over their final 8–12 cm of arc length so each visible end reaches the intended mint range before its return curl. Match gradient values across neighboring overlapping sections at the same location, and avoid shortening G_SPAN globally, which would create a more obvious horizontal band.
- **P3** [naturalness] *clay side, clay q34_back_right* - Body-facing inner locks and the smaller side-facing returns: Some secondary contours have short straight stretches and small directional corners that remain visible without the cel material. These weaken the otherwise rounded construction, particularly where a secondary lock crosses a broad primary surface.
  - Fix: Remove the INNER_LEFT seg=34 override or reduce it to 12–14 px, and use 16 section vertices on exposed SWEEP bends. Extend _resample() to insert rings when section width, thickness, or twist changes rapidly, even when the spine itself is nearly straight; the current length-and-turn criteria do not capture those shape changes.
- **P3** [fidelity] *back, q34_back_right* - Broad pale surface between the crown and the lower-lobe split: The middle back loses much of the reference's long internal strand rhythm, yet retains scattered short marks near the tips. The result is a largely blank upper mass followed by a visually busy lower fringe.
  - Fix: After cleaning the geometry and terminators, select a few continuous primary boundaries for deliberate internal lines. In outline_weights(), identify those boundaries by lock and arc-length range and fade their widths over several rings, rather than depending solely on the binary STEP_PX test and one-pass majority filter. Keep buried and flush secondary seams suppressed so the added lines describe the main flow rather than every loose part.
