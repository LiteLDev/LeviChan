# Codex review: gpt-6-astra

The back hair preserves Levi’s overall silhouette, but the rear three-quarter and clay views reveal a layered shell with exposed strip ends and flattened curl sections rather than a consistently rounded mass of hair. The most important correction is to resolve the actual depth ordering of the curtain, primary locks, and secondary locks before further tuning custom normals or outlines. The lower curls need fuller cross-sections and less crowded overlaps, while the broad upper mass needs more continuous strand flow into those curls. The pose sheet shows no clear back-hair collapse or tearing, although torso bends expose small separation and stiffness issues around the shoulder locks. Scores apply to the focused hair_back review; the supplied stills do not establish temporal stability or PMX physics behavior.

Scores: naturalness 5/10, animation 7/10, fidelity 6/10

## hair_back

- **P1** [naturalness] *clay back, clay q34_back_right, q34_back_right* - Lower central hair mass, where the straight secondary locks end above the curled tips: Several secondary locks emerge from the pale mass as blunt tongues and narrow wedge-shaped edges. The clay view makes their layered construction conspicuous, while the cel view reads as a ragged cut edge rather than continuous flowing hair. Their disappearance into neighboring surfaces is not consistently hidden.
  - Fix: Revise the terminal paths of MAIN B/C/E/G and WAVES in levi/parts/hair_back.py against the actual neighboring meshes. For ends intended to disappear, sink the entire terminal cross-section beneath its covering lock, starting over the last 6–10 cm, with an initial 0.5–1 cm burial margin. For exposed ends, continue the spine into a deliberate tapered free lock instead. Use a neighboring-surface clearance test rather than relying only on _layer() offsets and TUCK_PX.
- **P1** [naturalness] *clay q34_back_right, clay back, side* - Bottom curl cluster behind the waist, particularly the large loop and adjacent smaller returning tips: The curls read as stacked curved straps: broad faces turn into thin edges, and neighboring returns crowd together into an ambiguous knot. This is visible in clay, so shading changes alone will not restore convincing volume.
  - Fix: Give MAIN A/D/F and the curled SWEEP lock fuller sections through the bend: start by increasing their curl thickness ratios from 0.55 or the 0.38 default toward 0.7–0.8, with inner bulge around 0.85. Adjust hook pitch and tilt individually to separate overlapping return paths by roughly 1–2 cm where they should remain distinct. Preserve the large curl openings and taper the final quarter gradually; do not enlarge all curls uniformly.
- **P1** [naturalness] *back, q34_back_right, clay q34_back_right* - Broad rear surface from the crown to the lower shoulder-blade area: The upper hair reads as one smooth mantle, then abruptly breaks into numerous strips near its lower edge. The weak connection between the broad upper volume and the lower lock structure makes the rear three-quarter silhouette feel assembled from overlapping sheets.
  - Fix: Reshape MAIN and WAVES so three or four dominant rounded ridges continue from the upper back into the existing lower lobes. Introduce approximately 0.8–1.5 cm of gradual depth relief through the middle lengths, while recessing the backing curtain locally so it remains an internal support. Reduce TOP_WIDE from 1.3 toward 1.15 where excessive overlap erases the strand structure, and inspect the resulting clay turntable before changing volume normals.
- **P2** [naturalness] *q34_front_left, high, clay high* - Long outer locks behind both sleeves and their upward-pointing crescent tips: These locks have volume, but their long, nearly uniform faces and regular hook silhouettes still read like molded appendages. The transition from a broad diagonal strip to the narrow upturned tip is more mechanical than the softer, rolling hair in the reference.
  - Fix: Refine the second and third OUTER_LEFT paths with a gentle additional 1–2 cm depth excursion through the lower third, mirrored to the other side. Retain the existing transport frame, but distribute the twist over a longer section instead of concentrating the visible turn near the hook. Reduce width progressively before the lowest point so the crescent grows out of the lock rather than appearing attached to its end.
- **P2** [naturalness] *islands back, islands q34_back_right, islands side* - Rear strand boundaries, lower lock overlaps, and side-facing curl junctions: Small dark slivers interrupt otherwise broad pale hair surfaces and make the layered edges look chipped. The hair-region detector reports 12 islands in back and 15 in rear three-quarter; those totals include other hair, but several marked defects visibly lie on the back mass. Some are overlap-related shadows, while others coincide with abrupt local shading changes.
  - Fix: After correcting overlaps, inspect hair_back_side shadow contribution separately from the shell and noncasting locks. Split casting behavior by lock section if necessary so a buried upper portion does not cast a thin shadow onto its neighbor while the free curl still casts. In set_volume_normals(), blend the own-normal contribution continuously along the spine and around exposed curl sections; avoid solving the remaining defects by globally flattening the normals or disabling all hair shadows.
- **P2** [animation] *forward_bend q34_front_left* - Short shoulder lock protruding behind the upper back: The short lock separates visually from the longer mass as a small projecting fin during the forward bend. This is a minor continuity defect rather than a visible tear, but it exposes the independently moving layered construction.
  - Fix: Give this OUTER_LEFT shoulder lock an explicit attachment identity in the build output and use it in rig.py instead of depending solely on loose-part length/span classification. Match its root weights to the adjacent curtain over the first 4–6 cm, then blend into its own chain. Adjust its terminal depth only after posing so the intended overlap remains covered through forward_bend.
- **P2** [animation] *side_bend front, side_bend q34_front_left* - Upper outer hair on the raised-shoulder side: The upper lock follows the torso as a relatively stiff diagonal section while the lower hair hangs more vertically, leaving a tight change of direction near the shoulder. There is no obvious collapse, but the bend is less distributed than the lock's length suggests.
  - Fix: Preserve spine arc-length and lock identifiers as mesh attributes in clump(), then use those parameters to place and weight this lock's chain rather than fitting it only through height bands. Place an additional joint just below the shoulder transition and blend across at least three surrounding ring intervals. Keep the attachment stable and distribute the existing gravity counter-rotation across the next two segments.
- **P2** [fidelity] *sheet back versus render back, sheet side versus render side* - Lower half of the rear hair, from the central parting into the green curls: The reference has two flowing, overlapping lobes with a strong internal S rhythm and substantial mint-green curled ends. The render has a broader uninterrupted white center and a more horizontal row of exposed tips and loops. The overall outer silhouette conceals this internal design mismatch.
  - Fix: After establishing clean three-dimensional volumes, reposition MAIN B/C/D/E control points to carry the central parting diagonally into the two lower lobes. Stagger the main curl starts vertically by roughly 2–4 cm and redistribute width from the central sheet into those lobes. Tune grad_global() and per-lock gradient ranges afterward so green extends through the turning lower lengths rather than merely outlining the terminal loops.
- **P3** [naturalness] *back, q34_back_right* - Interior strand lines on the upper and middle rear mass: A few isolated short marks remain where a continuous strand boundary would be expected. These small line fragments reinforce the impression of surface scratches rather than intentional hair separation.

Remove very short isolated runs and terminate longer lines beneath a covering lock or at a genuine tip. Retain full silhouette outlines; avoid thickening every internal boundary.
