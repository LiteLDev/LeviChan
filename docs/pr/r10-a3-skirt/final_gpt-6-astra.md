# Codex review: gpt-6-astra

The skirt has a continuous, adequately sampled shell, but its lower half still reads as a stiff bell with localized dents and a projecting rear panel rather than softly supported fabric. The clearest naturalness problems are the shelf-like rear hem in side and three-quarter views, uneven lower-fold curvature in clay, and fragmented cel-shadow boundaries. Walk and squat expose an animation issue: the front panels lift and spread into broad shelves, with insufficient gathering between the raised cloth and the hanging sides. Fix the sampled surface and skirt-chain response before refining reference fidelity; the reference's deeper hem scallops and greater petticoat exposure should then be recovered through coordinated outer-layer shaping. These scores are skirt-focused, and the supplied Blender pose stills do not establish that the different PMX follow-and-physics setup will reproduce their behavior.

Scores: naturalness 5/10, animation 5/10, fidelity 6/10

## skirt

- **P1** [naturalness] *side; q34_back_right; clay side* - Rear lower third, from below the hair tips to the trailing hem at image-right in the side view: The rear panel projects into a long, comparatively straight wedge with an abrupt terminal edge. The clay view confirms that this is a surface-shape problem, not just a cel outline; it makes the skirt look like a stiff plate extending behind the wearer.
  - Fix: In levi/parts/skirt.py, revise the rear-sector radius field in _target() before _dense() consumes it. Start by pulling the last 20–25% of the rear profiles inward by approximately 2–3 cm, fading the correction smoothly around the rear quarters and upward. Replace the prolonged outward slope with a curved transition that becomes more vertical near the hem; make TGT_FLARE sector-dependent rather than flattening all folds with a global reduction. Keep section(), profiles() and front_surface_y() derived from the corrected field so dependent layers follow.
- **P1** [animation] *walk front; walk q34_front_left* - Front hem above the advancing thigh and its transition into the adjacent side panels: The stride lifts a wide area of the front skirt into a shelf, with a sharp change between the lifted section and the hanging cloth. This reads as neighboring panels being carried by the thigh rather than fabric redistributing its folds around it; there is no need to infer hidden leg penetration to identify the visible deformation problem.
  - Fix: Retain the existing 30 longitudinal rings and 360 azimuth samples. In levi/rig.py, adjust _skirt_drape() and its neighbor-gather calculation so thigh clearance is solved locally first, then adjacent sectors gather circumferentially without inheriting unnecessary root lift. Penalize changes in deformed ring-edge lengths and abrupt differences between adjacent segment tangents, not only differences in chain angles. Inspect _skirt_weights() at the lifted-to-hanging transition and soften its angular blend while retaining the four-influence PMX limit. Rebuild the drape tables after reshaping the rest skirt.
- **P1** [animation] *squat front; squat q34_front_left* - Lower front panel spanning both knees, especially the front-to-side hem transition: With both thighs raised, the front becomes a broad outward-facing apron and the adjacent hem forms a stiff ledge. The skirt opens around the pose, but does not develop enough local hanging folds to make the displacement look like cloth.
  - Fix: Add a bilateral-thigh correction to _skirt_drape() rather than relying solely on the combination of independent thigh solutions. Preserve necessary clearance at the upper segment, then let the lower two segments hang downward where the knee and shin envelopes permit. Couple the center-front and neighboring sectors through a ring-length constraint so gathering replaces outward spreading. Validate the correction on the shared skirt/petticoat envelope, and reproduce the intended response in PMX follow shares and joint limits rather than relying on a Blender-only correction.
- **P2** [naturalness] *clay front; clay q34_front_left; clay high* - Lower third of the front and front-quarter panels, just above the hem: Several folds end in swollen bumps and shallow transverse dents instead of flowing continuously down to the edge. These irregularities are visible in diffuse clay and make the fabric look molded or crumpled at isolated points.
  - Fix: Regularize the target radius residual in _target() along height as well as around the circumference. Replace bilinear target_radius() sampling with slope-continuous, bounded interpolation along rows, and blend across the points where the TGT_TUCK/TGT_FLARE slope clamp engages. As an initial local correction, reduce short residual bumps by about 30–50% over the last 8–12 cm while preserving the broad fold crests. Do not increase N_PHI globally; the current topology already samples these unwanted shapes densely.
- **P2** [naturalness] *high; q34_front_left; islands high; islands front* - Front-right fold-shadow boundary in image space, scattered mid-skirt slivers and small dark wedges along the hem: The pale fabric has jagged, intermittently detached shade fragments instead of a few deliberate fold shadows. The high-view dress mask reports 13 islands totaling 315 pixels, although that mask also includes the bodice and sleeves; the visible skirt slivers independently confirm the problem.
  - Fix: After correcting the geometry, make the Nsoft field used by _shade_normals() smooth along height as well as azimuth. Trial RELIEF_SHADE around 0.35–0.45 instead of 0.6, with the additional smoothing confined to noisy lower-fold areas and faded out at silhouette-defining creases. Recompute the hem-roll normal transition from the same field so small dark wedges do not reappear at the edge. Validate through intermediate camera angles with a fixed light as well as the supplied camera-relative lighting; do not move the global toon threshold merely to erase skirt artifacts.
- **P2** [fidelity] *sheet front versus render front; sheet back versus render back; sheet side versus render side* - Entire lower outline, particularly the lifted arches between the front and rear hem lobes: The outer hem has become a shallow, nearly continuous scalloped band. The reference alternates substantially deeper hanging lobes with lifted arches and turned edges, exposing much more pink underskirt. The current outer layer conceals that structure and makes the dress read as a simpler cone; low petticoat coverage supports this observation but does not by itself identify which layer caused it.
  - Fix: In target_hem() and the hem-row construction in _dense(), introduce a sparse, smooth correction curve that raises selected inter-lobe arches by an initial 2–4 cm while keeping the lowest lobes and waistband stable. Blend each change over a broad angular interval and the final 15–20% of the profile so it produces a rounded cloth turn rather than a notch. Recompute dependent petticoat clearance from shell() after the change, retaining roughly 0.5–1 cm separation where the two layers overlap. Recover the reference rhythm without tracing its painted contour into narrow fins.
- **P3** [fidelity] *render front; render back; q34_front_left* - Broad lower skirt panels between the main folds: Large panels remain nearly uniform ivory, so the pale mint turning planes contribute less depth than in the reference. This is separate from the small dark shade fragments: increasing shadow coverage alone would not restore the soft fabric coloration.
  - Fix: After the shape and shading fixes, drive the dress_mint attribute from the corrected broad fold field, with continuous height interpolation. Trial a modest increase of STREAK from 0.08 toward 0.12–0.16 on the broad turned-away faces, retaining the existing feathering and excluding tiny target residuals. Inspect the color attribute separately from lighting before adjusting Z_MINT, and keep the mint transition continuous rather than quantizing it into another hard band.
