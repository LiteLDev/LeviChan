# Codex review: gpt-6-astra

The skirt and petticoat form a continuous, readable outfit, but the oblique views still reveal a stiff bell-shaped overskirt and a petticoat that often reads as stacked scalloped strips rather than soft gathered fabric. The most important correction is to give the exposed petticoat folds rounded volume and improve how both layers drape over raised thighs in the squat. The skirt already has substantial sampling, so increasing mesh density globally would not address the main problems; changes should target fold profiles, hem transitions, and the deformation field. Reference fidelity is limited chiefly by the overskirt's shallow openings and the reduced, flattened pink clusters visible through them. These scores concern the focused garment assembly; still images demonstrate pose behavior but do not establish temporal stability or PMX physics performance.

Scores: naturalness 6/10, animation 6/10, fidelity 6/10

## petticoat

- **P1** [naturalness] *q34_back_right, clay side, clay q34_back_right* - Exposed pink tiers along the rear quarter and side hem: Several exposed folds read as flat, angular flaps with narrow pointed ends, while adjacent areas merge into broad flat bands. The clay views show that this is partly a shape problem, rather than only a toon-shadow artifact.
  - Fix: In petticoat.py _tier(), reshape the exposed lower portion of each band into a rounded hanging profile: ease the radial flare to near-vertical before the final PROFILE rings, and retain approximately 0.6–1.0 cm of radial gather depth where the tier is exposed. Broaden the notch transitions and add local samples where scallop curvature is greatest instead of increasing SPF everywhere. Check that _clamp_ring() preserves rounded crests and does not compress neighboring folds into flat flaps.
- **P1** [animation] *squat front, squat q34_front_left* - Both pink tiers beneath the lifted front and lateral overskirt: The ruffles inherit the overskirt's shelf-like rotation, so the exposed bands lie outward with it rather than retaining a hanging seam-to-hem drop. This makes the two materials behave like one rigid laminated garment.
  - Fix: Store each tier's seam-to-hem parameter from _tier() and use it to transition from the shared skirt-chain transform at the seam to a gravity-relaxed distal transform at the free edge. Keep attached seam vertices matched to the supporting core, and solve the free edge against the skirt and thighs with an initial 0.5–1.0 cm garment clearance. Carry the same behavior into PMX through supported bones and joint limits, then recheck the squat after export.
- **P2** [naturalness] *render front, render back, q34_front_left* - Broad exposed pink faces and the overlap between the two tiers: The exposed tiers frequently merge into an almost uniform pink ribbon, making the gathered volume and overlap hard to read. The low front and back shade shares support this observation; eliminating still more small islands would not restore the missing broad form.
  - Fix: In _finish(), make FOLD_SHADE vary with the tier profile: retain stronger smoothing near the hidden seam but preserve more of the actual gathered normals on broad exposed fold flanks. Tune the petticoat material threshold separately to produce connected flank shadows rather than thin crease shadows. Keep CAST_TIERS disabled initially so the adjustment does not reintroduce scalloped cast-shadow bands on the legs.
- **P2** [fidelity] *sheet front versus render front, sheet side versus render side* - Pink clusters beneath the front-quarter skirt openings and along the side hem: The reference has deeper, rounded clusters of overlapping ruffles, whereas the render mostly exposes a narrow continuous pink strip. Front and side visible pink areas are approximately 38 percent and 32 percent below the supplied reference measurements, respectively.
  - Fix: After restoring the skirt openings, retune cluster_rows() locally at those openings rather than lowering the entire petticoat. Increase exposed cluster depth by an initial 1–2 cm where the bowl and lift constraints permit it, and separate the two tier contours by approximately 0.5–1.0 cm in depth. Inspect the active HEM_FRONT, HEM_SIDE, and lift limits before increasing EXPOSE, because those limits can override the requested exposure.

## skirt

- **P1** [animation] *squat front, squat q34_front_left* - Lower front half of the skirt, above and outside both knees: The squat lifts a large portion of the skirt into an umbrella-like shelf. The silhouette stays intact, but the cloth rotates outward too uniformly instead of bending over the thighs and then falling from the contact region.
  - Fix: Adjust the skirt-chain drape solution so proximal segments provide thigh clearance while distal segments recover a downward world-space direction where collision allows. Keep the existing 1.2 cm DRAPE_CLEAR as a starting clearance, but add a hanging-direction penalty to the distal-joint solution and reduce neighbor lift outside the actual thigh-contact sectors. Preserve the skirt's existing longitudinal rings; this needs a different chain solution rather than more geometry.
- **P2** [naturalness] *q34_front_left, high, clay high* - Broad front panels between the waist and the lower lobes: Large areas read as smooth, stiff cone panels, with most of the fold character concentrated at the bottom edge. The resulting volume resembles a shaped shell more than cloth gathered from the waist.
  - Fix: In skirt.py profiles(), introduce broader, shallow fold relief through the middle third of the skirt, approximately 0.8–1.5 cm radially as a starting range, easing to zero at the waist attachment. Retune MID_CREST and fold_growth() so channels develop gradually down the panel rather than making the hem alone more corrugated. Keep the cross-sections smooth and preserve clearance from the petticoat.
- **P2** [naturalness] *q34_front_left, clay front, clay q34_front_left* - Outer lateral hem tips, particularly the image-left tip in the front-left review view: Some lateral lobes finish in thin projecting points rather than a soft fabric turn. They remain conspicuous from oblique angles even though the central hem is rounded.
  - Fix: Locally broaden the crest-to-trough transition in FOLDS and reduce the radial projection of the offending tip by roughly 0.5–1.0 cm. In profiles(), distribute its turn across the final 10–15 percent of skirt length instead of concentrating it at the edge. Retain the existing 0.3 cm HEM_THICK; increasing shell thickness would make the tip heavier without correcting its shape.
- **P2** [animation] *forward_bend q34_front_left, side_bend front* - Lower rear and raised-side skirt panels: The lower garment follows the bent upper assembly with limited gravitational relaxation. This is less severe than the squat, but the skirt and petticoat retain a conspicuously rigid shared tilt.
  - Fix: Keep the waist attachment on its existing body-weight transition and progressively counter torso pitch and roll toward the distal skirt-chain joints. Start with 30–50 percent counter-rotation at the hem, tapered to zero at the roots, then reduce it wherever it would violate body clearance. Apply the compatible relaxed transforms to petticoat support seams so the layers do not separate.
- **P2** [fidelity] *sheet front versus render front, sheet back versus render back* - Raised hem troughs on either side of the central front and back lobes: The render has a comparatively shallow, continuous scalloped outline. The reference's higher openings expose substantial pink ruffles and give the overskirt a more articulated folded silhouette.
  - Fix: Raise the relevant trough entries in FOLDS by an initial 1–2 cm while retaining broad rounded arches, then rebuild the petticoat from the resulting hem_terms(). Check the effect of _trough_floor() and SMOOTH_ROW_PX so the openings survive smoothing without becoming narrow spikes. Validate the change at intermediate turntable angles before fitting further to the orthographic outline.
