# Codex review: gpt-6-astra

The petticoat reads as a continuous layered garment, but its exposed fabric still looks like broad scalloped bands rather than softly gathered cloth, particularly in the three-quarter views. The most important shape problem is the abrupt transition between tucked sections and hanging panels: these create conspicuous pink flaps and sharp returns beneath the skirt. In the squat, the front tiers spread into a broad, nearly horizontal stack, losing their hanging volume; this needs attention before smaller silhouette and colour adjustments. The current shading is relatively free of small islands, so preserve that cleanliness while restoring rounded fold volume and clearer separation between tiers. These scores assess the petticoat and its interaction with the skirt; the supplied stills do not establish temporal stability or PMX physics behaviour.

Scores: naturalness 5/10, animation 6/10, fidelity 6/10

## petticoat

- **P1** [naturalness] *q34_front_left, q34_back_right, clay q34_back_right* - Exposed outer tier beneath the raised skirt troughs at the front and rear quarters: Several exposed sections form tall, broad pink flaps with sharply turning side boundaries. The three-quarter views reveal an angular panel or folded-fin appearance that is much less apparent in front view; the fabric does not turn continuously into the surrounding gathered band.
  - Fix: In petticoat.py, revise _tier() so the hem exposure limit and the minimum seam-to-hem length are solved together. The current max(r, _row(t.z) + MIN_LEN) can undo a requested upward tuck; move the local seam upward with the hem when necessary instead of forcing the exposed panel to remain long. Start by reducing tier0 EXPOSE_MAX from 42 to 28–32 px and blend the seam and hem adjustment across two to three neighbouring gathers. Recompute the intermediate PROFILE rings from that revised seam and hem, then inspect both three-quarter views to ensure the tuck does not end in a vertical fin.
- **P1** [animation] *squat front, squat q34_front_left* - Front half of both tiers, from the centre front to the outer quarters: The squat turns the exposed layers into a wide, nearly horizontal stack of pink strips. Their broad surfaces become exposed together and the hanging drop largely disappears, making the petticoat read as a rigid shelf carried upward by the skirt.
  - Fix: Adjust the petticoat's skirt-chain deformation in rig.py so collision-driven lift is concentrated above the exposed ruffles and the lower segment can rotate back toward a hanging orientation. If the shared skirt chains cannot preserve both garments, add petticoat-specific terminal helpers with a gravity-rest orientation and blend their influence from the lower half of PROFILE to the hem. Keep the seam attached to the existing chains, retain thigh clearance, and test the full standing-to-squat range rather than correcting only the final pose.
- **P2** [naturalness] *render front, q34_front_left, clay front* - Broad exposed faces of tier0 and tier1 beneath the front skirt hem: Most of the gathering is visible only as a small wavy bottom edge. The faces above it remain broad and smooth, so the two layers read as scalloped sheets rather than fabric gathered along a seam; the clay views show that this is partly a volume problem.
  - Fix: Increase the fold depth in TIERS gradually from 2.6/2.4 px toward approximately 3.5–4.5 px, maintaining the existing per-fold variation and staggered phases. In rings(), replace the jump from zero gathering at s=0 to a large SEAM_FOLD contribution at the first interior ring with a continuous short seam transition, adding a PROFILE ring near s=0.05 if needed. Keep the added depth inside the available skirt clearance and inspect the resulting cross-sections before changing shading.
- **P2** [animation] *walk q34_front_left* - Pink hem beneath the front-facing raised skirt edge: The walking pose gives the exposed tier a more pronounced projecting lip and an uneven drop relative to the layer beneath it. This is a small deformation problem in the supplied frame, but it makes the already broad ruffle panels look stiffer during leg motion.
  - Fix: Store a seam-to-hem parameter and rest azimuth on each tier in _tier(), then use those coordinates to keep the weight transition through each gathered panel consistent. Couple neighbouring tier seam weights to the same skirt-chain field while allowing the lower ruffle to bend independently; do not assign direct thigh weights. Check intermediate walk poses for local changes in panel height and hem direction before increasing mesh density.
- **P2** [fidelity] *sheet front versus render front, sheet side versus render side* - Visible pink ruffles around the lower skirt perimeter: The reference has more clustered, rounded ruffle lobes and a more interrupted pattern of pink exposure. The render presents longer continuous pink bands, especially across the front quarters, and the side silhouette carries more exposed pink area than the sheet. This changes the garment's texture and layering even though the overall character silhouette scores well.
  - Fix: After correcting the hanging panels, tune TIERS and tuck() locally rather than shrinking the complete petticoat. Raise the overexposed side-quarter sections by approximately 5–8 px, or 0.8–1.2 cm, using broad angular falloffs. Increase scallop rise selectively from 4–4.5 px toward 5–6 px and vary neighbouring fold sizes in small clusters, retaining round notches and the half-fold offset between tiers. Preserve the leg openings and verify the result from all turntable angles.
- **P3** [fidelity] *render front, render side, high* - Lit and shaded faces of the exposed pink tiers: The front and side ruffles are lighter and less shaded than the reference, while the high view already shows much more shade. Increasing darkness globally would therefore improve some views while making others heavy. The low island counts indicate that fine shadow speckling is not the main problem here.
  - Fix: Once the geometry is settled, test a modest increase of FOLD_SHADE from 0.3 toward 0.4 so broad fold turns contribute more to the normals. Tune only the petticoat material's lit and shade colours to reduce the washed-out pink, checking front, side, back, and high together. Keep CAST_TIERS disabled initially; do not restore full tier cast shadows merely to match the front shade fraction, since that would add scalloped shadows to the legs and darken the nested layers.
