# Codex review: gpt-6.1-sol

The skirt and petticoat form a continuous garment, but their lower volumes still read as a broad, shallow bell over stacked ruffle strips rather than softly folded fabric, especially in the clay and three-quarter views. The main naturalness problems are the pointed outer skirt lobes, weak separation between fold crests and valleys, and flattened petticoat exposure around the sides and rear quarters. Squatting exposes an animation problem: too much of the hem lifts together, producing a shelf around the bent legs instead of localized thigh clearance and hanging cloth. Prioritize reshaping the lower skirt and improving coordinated skirt–petticoat drape before adjusting reference colours and ruffle coverage. The supplied stills support a deformation review, but do not establish temporal stability or PMX physics behaviour.

Scores: naturalness 6/10, animation 6/10, fidelity 6/10

## petticoat

- **P1** [naturalness] *q34_back_right, clay q34_back_right, render side* - Exposed pink bands around the rear quarters and side hem: Several exposed ruffle sections read as flat, sloping strips with repeated vertical fold edges rather than rounded gathered clusters. At the rear quarter, the stacked strips form a conspicuous shelf underneath the green skirt.
  - Fix: In petticoat.py _tier(), reduce outward flare specifically through the exposed lower half of each band: test FLARE_POW around 1.1–1.2 instead of 1.35, and move the exposed hem inward by approximately 0.5–1.0 cm where it currently projects as a shelf. Preserve rounded gather depth and vary the local hem drop so the correction produces hanging folds rather than a flattened ribbon.
- **P1** [animation] *squat front, squat q34_front_left* - Pink tiers below the elevated front skirt hem: Both tiers follow the elevated skirt as a broad stacked assembly, exposing long pink bands with little downward relaxation. This makes the supporting fabric look rigid and amplifies the squat's shelf silhouette.
  - Fix: Give the tiers a lower-segment drape correction relative to the skirt chains, fading from zero at the sewn seam to a modest downward rotation at the hem. Start with 10–15 degrees of additional world-down relaxation in the squat, then limit it against the thighs and the outer skirt with approximately 0.5–0.8 cm interlayer clearance. Keep tier seams attached to the core and do not solve this by weighting the ruffles directly to the legs.
- **P2** [naturalness] *render front, q34_front_left, render back* - Lower scalloped edges of both pink tiers: The visible scallops are small and shallow enough to merge into a wavy ribbon, particularly across the front. The reference's rounded, individually legible ruffle clusters are largely lost even where the pink band is exposed.
  - Fix: In TIERS, test reducing the circumferential gather counts from 34/30 to approximately 28/26 and increasing scallop rise from 4.0/3.6 px to approximately 5.5/4.5 px. Keep SPF at 12 initially and retain smooth depth variation; inspect the oblique silhouette before adding samples or stronger fold shadows.
- **P2** [animation] *walk q34_front_left, forward_bend q34_front_left* - Front-quarter tier hems below the moving outer skirt: The tiers maintain nearly parallel spacing as the garment lifts, which makes the exposed pink layers look laminated. This is a smaller version of the squat problem and is noticeable during ordinary locomotion.
  - Fix: Parameterize tier weights by normalized seam-to-hem distance in addition to angle and height. Match the support weights exactly at each sewn seam, then gradually increase the lower segment's influence toward each tier hem so the tiers can bend independently without separating at their attachments. Validate this together with the squat correction rather than adding a separate pose-only offset.
- **P2** [fidelity] *sheet front versus render front, sheet side versus render side* - Ruffle clusters beneath the raised skirt troughs on either side of centre: The front and side show too little pink fabric and too few substantial clusters. The supplied region measurements support the visible deficit: rendered petticoat coverage is about 62% of the reference in front and 68% at the side, whereas the back already has comparable total coverage.
  - Fix: After correcting the skirt folds, adjust cluster_rows() exposure by azimuth rather than lowering the whole petticoat. Start by adding 6–10 px of local drop beneath the front-quarter troughs and 3–5 px at the visible side clusters; retain the existing central lift and back limits. Recheck the radial clamp because extra exposure must remain inside the skirt above its hem.
- **P2** [fidelity] *render front, render back* - Broad visible surfaces of the pink ruffles: The pink tiers read too pale and evenly lit, reducing the visual separation of overlapping folds. Front and back shade shares are approximately 0.21–0.22 against 0.38–0.39 in the sheet; this is more significant than the small-island count for this part.
  - Fix: Once the gathered shapes are corrected, tune the petticoat-specific base/shade pair toward a slightly deeper, less washed-out peach and adjust only its ramp threshold in small steps. Preserve CAST_TIERS=False initially, since re-enabling all tier shadows can introduce scalloped bands on the legs; recover broad fold shading through the tier normals and material before adding selective contact shadows.
- **P3** [naturalness] *islands back, islands q34_back_right* - Lower rear ruffle notches and overlapping hem edges: Small isolated dark fragments interrupt otherwise continuous pink scallops, especially along the rear hem. They are secondary to the shape issues but will read as chipped edges when the character turns.
  - Fix: Inspect the affected gather valleys and _hem_roll() normals after the shape changes. Blend the shading normals more strongly toward the smooth band only within the final profile rings, testing a local FOLD_SHADE of 0.45–0.55 instead of the current uniform 0.68, and keep the rolled-edge normals continuous. Preserve actual overlap shadows that describe layer contact.

## skirt

- **P1** [naturalness] *clay q34_front_left, clay back, q34_front_left* - Outer lateral hem lobes and the lower quarter of the skirt: The lateral lobes terminate in thin projecting points, while the cloth immediately above them forms broad, relatively flat faces. This makes the skirt read as a flared shell with cut tips when viewed obliquely; rounding the tiny hem cross-section has not rounded the larger cloth silhouette.
  - Fix: In skirt.py, reshape the affected FOLDS and profiles() over t=0.80–1.0: retract the projecting lateral tips by an initial 0.8–1.5 cm and distribute their radial turn across the last 4–6 existing rings. Broaden the circumferential transition into each trough rather than increasing HEM_THICK or HEM_FLARE; retain the thin rolled edge.
- **P1** [animation] *squat front, squat q34_front_left* - Front and lateral skirt panels above the bent thighs: The squat lifts a large portion of the skirt into a nearly continuous outward shelf. Clearance is being achieved at the expense of believable cloth drape: panels away from the thigh contact remain elevated with the contact panels.
  - Fix: In rig.py _skirt_drape(), narrow the initial neighbour-lift footprint by testing DRAPE_SPREAD at 65–75 degrees instead of 100, while retaining the 1.2 cm thigh clearance. Recompute clearance after neighbour blending and allow the lower chain segments to return toward world-down where they are clear of the thighs. Judge the result in both squat views before changing mesh density; the skirt already has 30 longitudinal stations.
- **P2** [naturalness] *clay front, clay q34_front_left, high* - Main fold channels from below the hips to the front hem: The lower skirt has broad balloon-like lobes but relatively weak intervening channels through much of its height. The result reads more like a moulded bell than fabric whose folds develop continuously from the waist.
  - Fix: Adjust fold_growth(), MID_RELIEF and MID_CREST so relief develops gradually through t=0.35–0.80 instead of relying on the final hem wave. As a starting shape change, add approximately 0.5–1.0 cm of crest-to-valley relief in selected main channels, with a broad smooth transition and no extra displacement at the waist. Avoid deepening every fold uniformly.
- **P2** [naturalness] *q34_front_left, high, islands q34_front_left* - Long shaded fold on the viewer-right front skirt panel: A narrow, pointed mint shadow runs down an otherwise broad pale panel and ends abruptly above the hem. Its hard cel boundary makes the fold look like an inset blade rather than a broad turn of fabric.
  - Fix: Broaden and round the geometric steep-face transition in _hem_terms_raw() and its radial offsets through the middle skirt, then recalculate smooth normals. If a pointed streak remains after the form correction, reduce STREAK locally and widen its feather with STREAK_SOFT; avoid changing the global dress ramp to hide a single fold.
- **P2** [fidelity] *sheet front versus render front, sheet back versus render back* - Raised hem troughs framing the pink ruffle clusters: The rendered hem is a comparatively continuous low wave, while the sheet has deeper raised openings between longer hanging lobes. Matching the overall silhouette has therefore retained the bell's outline while losing the internal skirt–petticoat rhythm.
  - Fix: Refit the relevant FOLDS trough heights with the visible dress–petticoat boundary as an explicit target, not only the union silhouette. Test raising selected front-quarter troughs by 8–12 panel px, blending over a broad angular interval, and evaluate the resulting openings at 45-degree views. Keep curvature and slope penalties so the openings do not become pointed fins.
