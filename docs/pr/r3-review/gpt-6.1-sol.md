# Codex review: gpt-6.1-sol

Levi has a recognizable overall silhouette, but the angled and clay views expose construction problems that the high orthographic silhouette IoUs conceal: flattened hair hooks, a stepped cap-to-back-hair transition, pinched skirt hems, and a petticoat that reads as stacked rigid shells. The first pass should repair these volumes and their sampling, then regenerate shading normals; palette or ramp changes alone will not resolve them. The pose sheet shows sharp compression of the hanging bodice ribbons during the squat and sleeve ties separating from the cloth during arm elevation, so those attachments need explicit weights derived from their supporting surfaces. Animation readiness is provisional because these stills do not establish twist behavior, collision recovery, or PMX playback. Once the forms and attachments hold up, the largest fidelity gains will come from opening the fringe around the eyes, restoring the back hair's flowing curls and green tips, and reducing the exposed petticoat.

Scores: naturalness 5/10, animation 5/10, fidelity 6/10

## hair_back

- **P1** [naturalness] *q34_back_right; clay back; clay q34_back_right* - back of skull, where the crown ends and the hanging clumps begin: The rounded crown terminates in a conspicuous stepped boundary above the hanging hair. Several clumps start with broad, nearly horizontal shoulders, making the hairstyle read as a cap placed over a separate curtain.
  - Fix: In hair_back.py, extend the hanging clump roots farther up the skull and bury their capped ends approximately 0.5–1 cm beneath the crown. Stagger the root heights and widen each clump gradually across its first 3–4 rings rather than exposing a broad root shoulder. Match the curtain's position and tangent to the crown before blending their shading normals.
- **P1** [naturalness] *q34_back_right; clay q34_back_right; turn_135* - lower back-hair tips, especially the large central and outer green hooks: The lower clumps become broad, squared ribbons with hooked ends. Their bends expose flat faces and abrupt changes in thickness, producing a cut-sheet appearance rather than continuous rounded locks.
  - Fix: In clump(), replace the strongly flattened lens profile on the terminal curls with a fuller elliptical section. Start by increasing the curl thickness-to-width ratio to approximately 0.4–0.5, using 10–12 vertices around those sections, and reducing SEG_PX from 16 to about 8 locally around tight bends. Taper over several rings before closing the tip, and vary the curl planes so the hooks do not all present broad faces in the back view.
- **P1** [naturalness] *q34_back_right; render back; turn_135; turn_225* - white-to-mint transition across the lower hanging mass: Several clumps change from pale hair to saturated mint across short, nearly straight boundaries. The repeated transition heights create a visible band across the hairstyle and reinforce the flat-ribbon construction.
  - Fix: Extend the per-clump gr intervals from the current roughly 65–70 panel pixels toward 110–140 pixels, then stagger their start and end rows by 10–20 pixels. Add spine samples through that transition so toon_grad interpolates across enough rings, retain the monotonic treatment through curls, and align the curtain gradient with the neighboring clumps.
- **P2** [fidelity] *render back; render side; sheet back* - lower half of the back hair, from waist to curled tips: The reference breaks the lower mass into flowing S-shaped locks with substantial green curls. The render instead presents a broad, comparatively straight pale curtain with smaller isolated hooks, covering more of the back dress and losing the reference's internal rhythm.
  - Fix: Reshape MAIN, SWEEP and WAVES so the center mass divides into several overlapping S-curves before the tips. Pull the backing curtain inside those clumps and shorten it where it fills the intended gaps between curls. Restore the green curl area by widening and curving the terminal sections in depth as well as width, while keeping rounded sections and clearance from the skirt.

## hair_front

- **P1** [naturalness] *q34_front_left; q34_back_right; high; turn_045* - both long outward-curving locks beside the sleeves: The long hooks read as thin, flat strips with abrupt upturned ends. At oblique angles their broad face collapses into a narrow edge, and the isolated hook silhouette resembles a rigid decorative blade rather than a hair clump.
  - Fix: Rework CURL_LOCK and its strand construction in hair_front.py: shorten the isolated terminal hook, spread its bend across the final third of the spine, and use a transported cross-section frame that rotates gradually with the curl. Increase thickness from 0.012 m toward 0.018–0.022 m through the main bend, tapering both width and thickness into the tip. Use 8–10 cross-section vertices instead of 6.
- **P2** [naturalness] *q34_front_left; high; clay q34_front_left* - fringe tips and the long locks immediately in front of the cheeks: The fringe separates into narrow pointed teeth, while the cheek locks have sharp planar ridges. The angled views emphasize these repeated wedges and give the face framing a rigid, serrated appearance.
  - Fix: In strand(), increase cross-section sampling from 6 to 8–10 for the bangs and cheek locks, and add longitudinal samples near the final taper. Replace the abrupt single-apex finish with two progressively smaller rings before the apex. Group neighboring bangs into a few broader primary clumps with smaller secondary tips, keeping the roots overlapped beneath the crown.
- **P2** [fidelity] *render front; render side; q34_front_left* - fringe above the eyes and locks beside the profile: The fringe hangs lower and splits into more evenly repeated spikes than the reference. In profile and three-quarter views the front locks obscure much more of the eye and cheek, weakening the open, soft expression of the sheet.
  - Fix: After rounding the clumps, revise the bangs() control-point table to lift the tips directly above each iris by approximately 1–1.5 cm while retaining the longer central accent. Move the cheek-lock paths outward by approximately 0.5–1 cm through eye and cheek height, and reduce their forward projection where they overlap the visible profile eye.

## petticoat

- **P1** [naturalness] *q34_front_left; q34_back_right; high; clay side* - side-facing pink tiers beneath the outer skirt: The petticoat exposes broad planar strips and tightly stacked scalloped shelves. Its side sections read as pleated plates rather than soft gathered fabric, and the high view turns most of the visible pink into a dense shadow band.
  - Fix: In petticoat.py, replace the four-ring RINGS profile with 7–9 rings, concentrating samples around the belly and return fold. Round the transition currently spanning height fractions 1.00 to 0.14, reduce the inward return from scale 0.90 where it creates a flat shelf, and stagger tier heights and radial positions by approximately 0.5–1 cm. Recalculate the elevation normals from the rounded profile rather than assigning the same -70-degree elevation across both return rings.
- **P2** [fidelity] *render side; render front; sheet side* - visible pink band below the outer skirt, especially at the front and rear of the side view: The pink underskirt is too exposed and visually heavy. The side-view region contains about 40% more petticoat pixels than the sheet, consistent with the visibly thick stacked band.
  - Fix: After rounding the tier profiles, raise the outer exposed tier edge approximately 1–2 cm at the front and rear and reduce tier height where multiple layers remain simultaneously visible. Recess inner tiers radially so they appear selectively between scallops rather than forming a continuous thick band. Preserve a soft irregular edge instead of uniformly shrinking the entire petticoat.

## skirt

In clay, several troughs form hard wedges; in cel, their outlines make the cloth look chipped or cut from stiff material.

Reduce curl amplitude where adjacent rolled sections crowd together.

## rig

- **P1** [animation] *squat front; squat q34_front_left* - waist bow and long green bodice ribbons over the center-front skirt: The long ribbons compress into sharp zigzags during the squat, with an abrupt change immediately below the waist bow. They no longer follow a continuous hanging path and look buckled against the skirt.
  - Fix: Replace the ribbons' general height-based body-to-skirt envelope in rig.py with attachment-specific weights. Keep the knot and bow loops on the waist bone, then transfer weights to each tail from the corresponding outer-skirt surface using barycentric interpolation. Blend along arc length over the first 4–6 cm below the knot and add tail samples at approximately 1 cm intervals through that transition. Maintain a 3–5 mm offset outside the deformed skirt.
- **P1** [animation] *arms_up front; arms_up q34_front_left* - green sleeve ties and long tails along the outer sides of both raised sleeves: The ties lift away from the sleeve into long diagonal prongs. Their deformation differs visibly from the balloon underneath, exposing that the attachment and supporting cloth are being driven independently.
  - Fix: In rig.py, identify the sleeve tie meshes separately from the balloon. Give each knot the weights of its attachment patch, and transfer the proximal tail weights from the nearest sleeve surface instead of independently evaluating arm and drape envelopes. Fade to dedicated tail chains only beyond the attached section. Preserve the rest clearance of a few millimeters through the arms_up and elbow-bend poses.
- **P2** [animation] *squat front; squat q34_front_left* - front skirt and petticoat over the bent thighs: The squat produces a broad, lifted shelf of petticoat while the skirt's front lobes bunch unevenly above it. The layers retain much of their standing bell shape instead of forming a coordinated drape over the bent legs.
  - Fix: In rig.py, replace the unconditional full center-front thigh follow with a reduced or clamped bend response; start by testing FOLLOW_FRONT around 0.6–0.7. Derive corresponding skirt and petticoat sector weights from one shared parameterization so their separation stays consistent. Add a knee-bend corrective that lifts and spreads the front cloth over the thighs while relaxing the side sectors, rather than rotating the whole front shell as a rigid flap.
- **P2** [animation] *head_left front; head_left q34_front_left* - face-framing locks between the jaw, neck and shoulders: When the head turns, the near cheek lock sweeps across the face and the longer locks remain crowded against the neckline. The resulting face occlusion is much stronger than in the rest pose and makes the head turn difficult to read.
  - Fix: Separate the short cheek-lock roots from the long side-lock chain weights in rig.py. Keep the short face-framing clumps fully attached to the head, then introduce the partial head-follow response gradually below the jaw for the longer locks. Add a turn corrective that pushes the near long lock approximately 1–2 cm outward from the cheek and neck at the tested 45-degree turn, with a smooth falloff to zero.
