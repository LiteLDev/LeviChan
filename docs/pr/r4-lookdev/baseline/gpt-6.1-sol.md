# Codex review: gpt-6.1-sol

Levi has a strong overall silhouette, but the cel treatment still exposes angular construction in the legs, hair and skirt instead of unifying those forms into a believable character. The main lookdev problem is the shape of the shadow boundaries: acceptable regional shade percentages conceal triangular skin patches, abruptly shaded hair sections and flat-looking cloth. First isolate cast shadows from normal-driven shading, correct the underlying normal fields and hair colour transitions, then retune thresholds and palette colours independently. Preserve the two-tone treatment, but use coherent volume normals and restrained contact shadows rather than compensating for malformed shadow shapes with lighter shade colours. Animation readiness remains provisional because the supplied poses show deformation snapshots, while the hard shading boundaries are particularly sensitive to the changing surface orientations those poses produce.

Scores: naturalness 5/10, animation 5/10, fidelity 6/10

## body

- **P1** [naturalness] *clay front, clay back, clay q34_front_left* - both knee transitions and the upper-to-lower calf surfaces: The clay render already shows sharply bounded changes in the leg shading around the knees, so the cel ramp is amplifying an underlying surface or normal problem. The visible transition reads as a joint between separate forms rather than a continuous limb.
  - Fix: In body.py, inspect the generated leg rings for abrupt tangent changes and sharp edges introduced by common.shade_smooth(obj, 75.0). Clear sharp flags across the skin's knee rings, retain smooth normals through the full leg, and resample the profile with continuous first derivatives. If the transition still kinks, add two intermediate rings on each side of the knee, roughly 1–1.5 cm apart, while preserving the kneecap and calf volumes.

## hair_back

- **P1** [naturalness] *clay side, clay q34_back_right, q34_back_right* - outer hanging locks behind the shoulders and the large lower curls: Several locks expose broad slab-like faces and abrupt section changes before entering the curls. The clay views show that these are actual form changes, and the cel treatment converts them into large flat shade blocks. Adjusting the ramp alone will leave the cardboard-like profile intact.
  - Fix: In hair_back.py, round the affected lock sections before increasing overall mesh density: give their minor radius approximately 25–35% of their half-width and use 10–12 vertices around sections that are visible edge-on. Interpolate the section frame continuously into each hook, with at least four intermediate sections across the root-to-curl transition. Recompute the analytic own normals from those rounded sections before blending with the proxy-volume normals.

## lookdev

- **P1** [naturalness] *q34_front_left, high, render back* - exposed thighs immediately below the skirt and the sides of both knees: Skin shading breaks into angular wedges with abrupt corners instead of following the rounded legs. The front three-quarter view has a prominent diagonal patch across the upper legs, and the back view shows pointed transitions around the knees. These boundaries make the limbs read as assembled polygonal sections; the back skin shade share of 0.31 versus 0.18 in the sheet also shows that this is more than a small colour discrepancy.
  - Fix: Use render.py --no-shadows on these same views to separate skirt-cast boundaries from the leg terminators. Keep the skin threshold at 0.30 initially; correct the leg normal discontinuities before tuning it. For the cast component, test a sun angular size of 2–4 degrees with sufficient shadow samples, and introduce a skin-only ramp half-width around 0.02 rather than changing the global RAMP_SOFTNESS. Retain whichever combination removes the angular patches without washing out the chin and skirt contact shadows.
- **P1** [naturalness] *render side, q34_front_left, q34_back_right* - side hair behind the temple and shoulder, and lower back-hair clumps above the curls: Broad grey-green blocks terminate abruptly against nearly white hair, while neighbouring locks switch tone independently. The side hair reads as layered cut panels, and the back three-quarter hair loses its rounded flow across the lower clumps. Matching front hair shade coverage does not resolve this: side hair remains 0.51 shaded against 0.37 in the sheet, while back hair falls to 0.24 against 0.37.
  - Fix: Keep the hair threshold at 0.42 while repairing the shading field. Have the front and back hair normal builders sample a continuous scalp-to-curtain proxy volume, and blend toward each lock's own normals gradually along its length. Avoid a fixed forward bias dominating the side locks. Tune the resulting normal field across front, side and back together; do not raise or lower the shared hair threshold to correct only one view.
- **P1** [naturalness] *render back, render side, q34_back_right* - white-to-mint transition across the lower hanging hair and curl roots: The mint transition forms a conspicuous belt across several locks, with relatively abrupt steps where one lock overlaps another. Colour change and a stronger local shadow response occur in the same area, so the tips look like separate green components rather than a gradual change within continuous hair.
  - Fix: Decouple gradient colour from shading-normal variation. In hair_back.py, stop using the same r0/r1 interval to drive both toon_grad and the increase toward LOW_KEEP; give those effects separate intervals. Spread the colour transition over approximately 12–18 cm of strand arc length, stagger its start between locks, and keep the normal blend continuous through the curl roots. Preserve the continuous palette interpolation already implemented in toon.py.
- **P2** [naturalness] *q34_front_left, high, render front* - large front skirt panels and the outward-facing sleeve balloons: Large cloth surfaces remain almost uniformly pale, interrupted by narrow angular mint or shadow slivers. This flattens the skirt and sleeves even where the clay render shows substantial volume. Dress shade coverage is only 0.18–0.19 in the front and front three-quarter views, versus 0.23 in the sheet's front, but boundary placement matters more than simply increasing that percentage.
  - Fix: Separate dress and sleeve tuning instead of leaving all four cloth keys at 0.48. After smoothing the fold normals, test dress thresholds of 0.50–0.54 while holding sleeves at 0.48. Place broad shadow turns on the recesses between folds through continuous fold normals; do not use the mint colour attribute as a substitute for form shading. Compare the high view at each step to prevent the adjustment from turning the whole skirt underside into one block.
- **P2** [fidelity] *render front, render side, render back* - green sleeve bows, leaf ornaments and thin hanging ties: The green decorations read as heavier and duller than the painted reference, while the lit/shade distribution differs in opposite directions by view. Front green is only about 0.36 shaded against 0.57 in the sheet, yet back green is about 0.69 against 0.59. Darkening the palette has not recreated the reference's leaf modelling; it has reduced the distinction between the ornament faces.
  - Fix: Retune spec.PALETTE_TUNING for green and leaf independently of the ramp. Start by moving green base from #28753A toward #2D8040 and leaf base from #4AA460 toward #55AE69, leaving shade colours unchanged for the first comparison. Give leaf its own threshold rather than sharing 0.50 with green, and sweep it in 0.03 increments only after checking the ornament normals. Judge visible leaf faces and bow lobes separately from the thin ties, whose region measurements are sensitive to alignment.
- **P2** [fidelity] *render front, q34_front_left, q34_back_right* - shoe toe boxes, outer quarters and heel counters: The shoes carry broad dark areas that merge the toe and upper into a nearly solid green mass. The supplied shade statistics put front shoes at roughly 0.40–0.46 shaded versus 0.26 in the sheet, with 0.44 in the front three-quarter view. This makes the footwear look heavier and obscures its curved construction.
  - Fix: Add an explicit shoe entry to RAMP_BY_KEY and test 0.20–0.25 instead of the inherited 0.30. Keep sole tuning separate and preserve contact darkening at the foot opening and heel. Check a no-shadow comparison before changing the palette so that shadow coverage is not corrected by brightening every shoe surface.
- **P3** [fidelity] *render front, high, q34_back_right* - crown strand seams, pale skirt hem and layered cuff borders: Line emphasis is uneven: crown separations create conspicuous thin grey scratches, while some pale cloth borders have little separation from adjacent surfaces. The uniform 1.25-pixel hull setting does not give the same apparent weight on overlapping hair clumps and open cloth edges.
  - Fix: Use the existing add_outline width parameter per object: test 0.7–0.9 reference pixels for crown and small overlapping hair pieces while retaining approximately 1.25 pixels on the main silhouette. For missing cloth borders, add a restrained explicit border curve or use selected Freestyle open-border edges; avoid enabling crease lines indiscriminately across the model.

## skirt

- **P1** [naturalness] *clay front, clay q34_front_left, high* - lower outer-skirt fold troughs and the turned hem immediately above the pink petticoat: The hem forms repeated angular notches with thick, bevel-like corners. These remain visible without outlines and create narrow, hard shadow strips in the cel views, making the garment resemble a rigid moulded shell instead of lightweight cloth.
  - Fix: In skirt.py, soften the trough profile and the transition into LIP_A rather than smoothing away the main folds. Add intermediate samples around each trough and two transition rings before the rolled lip; keep curvature continuous as the cloth turns under. Reduce the apparent rolled-edge thickness toward approximately 2–3 mm and restrict sharp edges to the actual inner hem boundary, then regenerate smooth normals across the outer fold-to-lip transition.
