# Codex review: gpt-6-astra

Levi's overall silhouette is close to the sheet, but the lookdev still exposes angular construction and creates conspicuous patches of shadow on otherwise soft forms. The most urgent shading problems are the skirt-shaped shadows across the thighs, the broad grey side-hair mass, and the abrupt mint transitions on individual curls. Fix shadow placement and the underlying hair normals before further matching aggregate shade percentages; those percentages currently hide substantial differences in where the shading lands. The pose images also expose small, distracting skin-shadow fragments around the shoulders, while static contact sheets cannot establish temporal stability or PMX shading parity. Preserve the two-tone treatment, but make its boundaries describe coherent volumes and use more selective outline thickness.

Scores: naturalness 5/10, animation 5/10, fidelity 7/10

## hair_back

- **P1** [naturalness] *render back; q34_back_right; clay q34_back_right* - lower curtain, large bottom curls and side locks extending beyond the sleeves: The mint colour begins in short, uneven bands on several locks, while the clay view also reveals broad flattened curl surfaces. These combine with the shared volume normals to make the ends read as painted bent strips rather than rounded hair. A material threshold change cannot repair the visible cross-sections.
  - Fix: In hair_back.py, spread each lock's toon_grad transition over roughly 12–18 cm of arc length and sample it over at least 8 rings, rather than allowing a short row-based transition to dominate a curl. In set_volume_normals(), fade from the curtain's volume normal toward 70–100% strand-local normals over the final 8–12 cm of each free curl. Round the visibly flat curl cross-sections and add samples at the tight bends before tuning the global hair ramp.

## lookdev

- **P1** [naturalness] *render back; q34_back_right; clay front; clay back* - exposed upper thighs immediately below the petticoat: The skirt casts broad, sharply cut shadow patches across the thighs, with conspicuous diagonal and scalloped lower edges. In clay these resemble dark shorts; in cel they become angular pink patches that interrupt the leg volumes. Their presence in both treatments points to cast-shadow placement rather than simply an overly dark skin palette.
  - Fix: In render.py, compare these exact views with cast shadows disabled to isolate the skirt contribution, then sweep SUN_ELEVATION_DEG from 12 toward 25–35 degrees while retaining the current azimuth. Choose the lowest elevation that confines the skirt shadow nearer the upper thigh without lengthening the face shadow excessively. Keep useful contact shadows; do not try to remove fully occluded patches by lowering the skin ramp threshold, since zero illumination remains below any positive threshold.
- **P1** [naturalness] *render side; q34_front_left; q34_back_right* - back-hair curtain behind the shoulder and the outer swept locks: The hair alternates between a large, nearly uniform grey side slab and an almost unshaded white rear curtain. The shade boundaries cut across the hair mass in angular patches rather than consistently explaining the locks' curvature. Side hair has 51% shade versus the sheet's 37%, while the back has only 24% versus 37%; changing one global hair threshold cannot resolve both directions.
  - Fix: Keep toon.py's hair threshold at 0.42 while correcting the hair normal field, then retune against side, back and both three-quarter views together. Use temporary normal-visualization and no-cast-shadow renders to separate custom-normal boundaries from cast shadows. Avoid increasing the global hair threshold to fill the back, which would further darken the side; retain the two-tone ramp and require continuous, curved shade boundaries before adjusting palette contrast.
- **P2** [naturalness] *high; arms_up q34_front_left; forward_bend q34_front_left* - upper chest, exposed shoulders and inner upper arms: Small isolated shadow slivers on the chest and narrow, hard skin wedges around the raised shoulders read as surface defects. The hard ramp gives these minor normal or occlusion changes the same visual emphasis as a deliberate anatomical shadow.
  - Fix: Extend _tune_ramp() in levi/toon.py to accept per-key softness and test a skin-only half-width of 0.02–0.04, leaving the cloth and hair ramps hard. Compare with no-shadow renders before adopting it: fragments caused by geometry or full occlusion need their source corrected rather than a wider ramp. Validate the selected skin ramp in the PMX toon texture as well, so the Blender preview is not the only improved output.
- **P2** [naturalness] *render back; q34_back_right; q34_front_left* - hair strand overlaps, skirt hem notches and layered cuff borders: Repeated internal contours accentuate the hair's separate strips and the skirt's angular hem construction. Fine seams and overlap edges receive enough line weight to compete with the outside silhouette, giving the pale forms an assembled, cutout appearance.
  - Fix: Use the existing width argument of toon.add_outline() selectively: start with 0.0008–0.0012 m on internal hair layers and small overlapping trim, retaining approximately 0.00194 m on the major silhouette meshes. Add a vertex-group thickness mask where one mesh contains both silhouette and internal overlaps. Check the rear curls and cuffs at final output size, and carry the same thickness distinctions into PMX edge settings.
- **P2** [fidelity] *render front; render side; render back* - green shoulder ornaments, leaf decorations and bodice ties: The greens read as dark, muted blocks compared with the more luminous leaf forms in the sheet. Their colour error is consistently high, approximately dE 16–17. The front and side already have less shade coverage than the reference, so reducing shade coverage further would not restore the intended colour.
  - Fix: In spec.PALETTE_TUNING, test moving the green and leaf bases partway back toward SHEET_PALETTE: start with green base #2B803C and leaf base #56B36C. Evaluate the shade colours separately in close views of the shoulder leaves rather than shifting every green key together. Hold RAMP_BY_KEY constant during the palette comparison, then adjust thresholds only after the lit colours are satisfactory.
- **P3** [fidelity] *render front; q34_front_left; high* - large skirt panels between the waist and hem: The broad skirt surfaces are comparatively blank and pale, with less fold shading than the sheet. Dress shade coverage is 18% versus 23% in front and 23% versus 30% at the back, and the current lightened dress shade further reduces the folds' visual depth.
  - Fix: After the hem geometry is corrected, test reverting only the dress shade override from #D5E2D4 toward an intermediate #D1E0D2 in spec.PALETTE_TUNING. Keep sleeve colours separate. If the large folds still lack definition, sweep only the dress threshold from 0.48 to 0.50–0.52 and reject settings that produce narrow triangular islands at the hem.

## skirt

- **P1** [naturalness] *q34_front_left; q34_back_right; clay front; clay high* - outer dress hem, especially the side notches and central front lobe: The hem has angular, squared-off turns and short folded-looking rim segments that remain visible without outlines. The cel ramp and hull lines amplify these into alternating bright and dark chips around the edge, preventing the skirt from reading as soft cloth.
  - Fix: In skirt.py, add two or three interpolated circumferential samples through each abrupt hem turn and blend the hem height and radial displacement with a cubic curve. Add a supporting ring approximately 0.5–1 cm above the edge so its normals do not jump directly from the main fold to the rim. Keep the existing large fold placement, but remove the short planar ledges before adjusting dress shading or outline width.
