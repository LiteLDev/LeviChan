# Codex review: gpt-6-astra

The current shading exaggerates the model's segmented construction: angular hair shadow boundaries, chipped skirt folds, and broad scalloped shadows on the thighs prevent the character from reading as a softly formed volume across angles. The strongest problems are visible in both cel and clay renders, so changing the global threshold or light alone will not resolve them. The code already supports continuous palette gradients, custom hair normals, selective shadow casting, and variable outline widths; the next pass should use those controls locally before retuning palette values. Animation readiness remains provisional: the posed stills show conspicuous shadow fragmentation around bent cloth, but they cannot establish temporal stability or MMD shading parity. Prioritize coherent normals and shadow silhouettes, then reduce internal line clutter and restore the reference's restrained colour separation.

Scores: naturalness 5/10, animation 5/10, fidelity 6/10

## hair_back

- **P1** [naturalness] *side, q34_back_right, clay back, clay q34_back_right* - mid-length rear locks, outer hanging strips, and the transition into the lower mint curls: The back hair exposes broad flattened strips with angular shadow wedges and abrupt changes where straight lengths become curls. The clay views retain these sectional breaks, so they are not solely a palette problem. The lower green region also becomes a heavy, fairly uniform band instead of following the individual locks gracefully.
  - Fix: In hair_back.py, refine add_lock sampling around high-curvature transitions and interpolate the cross-section frame continuously into each curl. Smooth the own-normal share along each lock before set_volume_normals, especially where it approaches CURL_KEEP, rather than allowing a rapid change from the shared volume normal. Replace row-driven gradient accumulation in curling regions with a monotonic arc-length parameter and spread the mint transition over several longitudinal sections. Preserve enough cross-section depth for the outer locks to remain rounded at three-quarter angles.

## lookdev

- **P1** [naturalness] *front, q34_front_left, high; 05_islands* - outer skirt folds and hem returns, sleeve ornaments, bangs and side locks: Small detached shadow slivers make the pale surfaces look chipped rather than smoothly cel shaded. Front has 67 islands, including 30 on the dress; q34_front_left has 62. Their concentration around folds and strand transitions is more important than their total pixel area. The pipeline notes also report that most survive disabling cast shadows, so shadow-map filtering is not the primary remedy.
  - Fix: Keep RAMP_SOFTNESS at 0 initially. In toon.py, add a diagnostic material path that displays the pre-ramp intensity and a second path that excludes cast shadows, then use these to identify and repair the contributing surface normals in the affected builders. Smooth normals across continuous cloth surfaces while preserving actual folded edges; do not smooth across disconnected layers. Re-render all six views after each local repair and require the visible isolated slivers to disappear without removing broad form shadows.
- **P1** [naturalness] *back, q34_back_right, high* - backs of thighs immediately below the petticoat; upper knees in the high view: The skirt assembly projects a large, jagged shadow apron onto the legs. Its repeated scallops and abrupt lateral steps dominate otherwise simple skin shading and make the thighs appear patched. Back skin shade coverage is 0.31 versus 0.18 in the reference; the visible shape of that shadow is the larger problem.
  - Fix: Separate the detailed ruffle appearance from the shadow silhouette. Extend render.py's shadow handling to support a shadow-only caster supplied by the petticoat builder, using a smooth continuous underside envelope. Preserve the outer skirt's broad cast shadow and retain visible tier shading on the petticoat. Test the receiver result on the thighs in back, high, walk, and squat views rather than lowering the global skin threshold.
- **P2** [naturalness] *back, q34_back_right, q34_front_left* - long internal boundaries down the back hair and doubled edges around the skirt hem: Internal strand borders and hem returns receive nearly the same visual emphasis as the outside silhouette. Together with the stepped shading, this makes the hair read as assembled strips and the skirt edge as a rigid folded shell. A uniform hull width cannot provide the reference's quieter internal separation.
  - Fix: Use the existing add_outline vertex_group support to taper hull thickness at buried strand overlaps and inner hem returns. Start those regions at 25–40% of their current width, blend back to full width over adjacent rows, and retain full thickness at exposed outer edges and curl silhouettes. Avoid globally thinning outlines, which would also weaken the hands and outer dress contour.
- **P2** [animation] *squat q34_front_left, forward_bend q34_front_left, arms_up q34_front_left* - folded sleeve cuffs, compressed skirt folds, and hair beside the shoulders: The posed renders contain narrow, irregular shade patches at compressed folds and overlapping hair. With RAMP_SOFTNESS = 0, small changes in these normals can switch a whole patch between tones. The stills demonstrate the fragmentation, although actual flicker cannot be confirmed without consecutive frames.
  - Fix: After repairing the implicated normals, render short interpolations into squat, arms_up, and forward_bend with a fixed world-space light as well as the current camera-relative light. If clean geometry still produces distracting threshold chatter, add per-key softness in toon.py and test a small 0.01–0.02 half-width on pale cloth and hair only. Implement the equivalent transition in the exported toon textures before accepting the change for PMX.
- **P2** [fidelity] *side, back, q34_back_right* - white lengths and mint lower third of the back hair: Hair shade coverage is inconsistent across views: the side is more shaded than the sheet, while the back is less shaded. Visually, the rear crown and central lengths flatten into a large pale area while the side becomes a broad grey-green slab. A single increase or decrease to the hair threshold would improve one view at the expense of another.
  - Fix: Leave the shared hair threshold near its current 0.42 while correcting the back-hair volume normals. Then compare side and back together when adjusting the existing hair and hair_tip palette entries in spec.PALETTE_TUNING. Preserve a readable continuous shadow on the rear mass instead of compensating for misplaced shadow by further lightening its shade colour. Use interior colour error and visible shadow placement together, rather than matching shade share alone.
- **P2** [fidelity] *front, side, q34_front_left* - leaf clusters at the upper arms and chest, ankle leaves, and green shoe uppers: The green ornaments look comparatively flat in front and side views, while the front shoes have a conspicuous dark area. The metrics support these separate tendencies: green shade share is approximately 0.35–0.46 against 0.57–0.70 in the sheet, whereas front shoe shade share remains about 0.40 against 0.26. Treating all green surfaces as one palette problem would push them in conflicting directions.
  - Fix: Tune the existing green, leaf, and shoe keys independently. After the leaf normals are cleaned, test green and leaf thresholds in small increments around 0.50, checking the already dark back-facing clusters before increasing them. For shoes, first compare with cast shadows disabled to determine why the front remains dark at a 0.10 threshold; correct that source before lowering the threshold further. Retune base and shade colours only after shadow placement is stable.
- **P3** [fidelity] *front, side, back* - pink petticoat tiers beneath the outer dress: The pink tiers read brighter and more candy-like than the reference's muted warm fabric. The interior colour error remains roughly 7–8 despite reasonably similar shade coverage in side and back views, so some of the mismatch is colour rather than simply insufficient shadow.
  - Fix: Adjust the existing petticoat palette entries in spec.PALETTE_TUNING using samples from broad tier interiors, excluding outlines and contact shadows. Reduce the excess brightness and saturation of the base first, then adjust the shade independently. Keep the reference classifier on SHEET_PALETTE so this tuning does not change the comparison target.

## petticoat

- **P1** [naturalness] *side, q34_front_left, clay side, squat q34_front_left* - stacked pink hem tiers and their underside: The ruffles form bulky, repetitive rounded lobes with abrupt dark seams. In the squat view the stacked tiers become an especially dense horizontal band. This construction contributes directly to the oversized scalloped shadows on the skin and makes the cloth read as padded trim.
  - Fix: In petticoat.py, reduce ruffle cross-section depth and round the fold transitions without inflating every scallop into a tube. Introduce controlled variation in tier spacing while maintaining clearance through the squat pose. Supply a smooth, skirt-weighted shadow envelope for the lookdev caster path and disable detailed ruffle casting only once replacement tier shading is verified; simply switching all petticoat shadows off would remove needed depth between layers.

## skirt

- **P1** [naturalness] *q34_front_left, high, clay front, clay q34_front_left* - front-side hem lobes, especially the stacked return edges at image-left: The hem has angular notches, doubled lips, and sharply pinched fold bottoms. These create both visible polygonal silhouette changes and many of the detached dress shade islands. In clay the lower dress reads as a thick folded shell rather than flexible fabric, so shader smoothing alone would conceal rather than resolve the problem.
  - Fix: In skirt.py, soften the crest-to-trough and lip transitions in the hem surface function and add sampling specifically around the tight return arcs. Make fold depth decay smoothly into each rounded lobe instead of ending at a short, steep return. Keep normals continuous over the curved fabric and split them only at a genuine underside turn. Check the Solidify thickness against the rebuilt curvature, then reduce the inner-return outline using an outline vertex group.
