# Codex review: gpt-6.1-sol

The model's main lookdev weakness is fragmented shading: pale hair and cloth acquire thin wedges, isolated flecks, and abrupt shade boundaries that make otherwise continuous surfaces look chipped or folded from card. The clay views confirm that some of these breaks originate in the geometry, so increasing shadow filtering or changing the global ramp alone will not resolve them. Prioritize coherent shading normals, smoother hair-tip colour transitions, and simpler cast-shadow silhouettes on the legs and skirt while preserving contact shade between garment layers. Then tune material-specific shade coverage and palette contrast; the strong silhouette IoU does not capture these interior shading problems. The pose images expose additional shading stress, but still images cannot establish temporal stability or verify the exported PMX appearance.

Scores: naturalness 5/10, animation 5/10, fidelity 7/10

## hair_back

- **P1** [naturalness] *clay side, clay q34_back_right, q34_front_left* - long outer locks beside the upper arms and descending toward the lower curls: Several locks expose broad, thin blade-like surfaces and sharply changing cross-sections at oblique angles. The clay render shows actual plate-like form, so palette and custom normals cannot make these pieces convincingly rounded.
  - Fix: In hair_back.clump(), increase the minor-axis thickness of the exposed long locks to roughly 0.25–0.35 of their width through the middle length, tapering gradually toward the tips. Use approximately 10–12 cross-section vertices on these visible locks and parallel-transport the section frame to avoid abrupt turns. Recompute the analytical own normals from the revised sections and retain only a modest envelope-normal blend on their exposed edges.

## lookdev

- **P1** [naturalness] *front, q34_front_left, high, 05_islands* - pale skirt panels, neckline, sleeve folds, and hair clump edges: Numerous detached shade flecks and narrow slivers interrupt broad pale surfaces. The front contains 30 dress islands and the front three-quarter view contains 19; visibly, these read as chips and creases unrelated to the intended large folds. The documented no-shadow comparisons retain most islands, so shadow-map resolution is not the primary cause.
  - Fix: Keep the global two-tone ramp and repair the normal fields feeding it. Add a reusable helper for transferring normals from a smooth, simplified shading surface to selected vertices, excluding genuine cloth rims and hair undersides. Apply it to the flagged broad surfaces, blend back toward geometric normals over two or three neighboring loops, and remove sharp-edge splits inside each continuous panel. Compare the same six views with shadows both enabled and disabled; require removal of the visible isolated patches without flattening the main fold shade.
- **P1** [naturalness] *side, q34_front_left, q34_back_right* - lower white back hair immediately above the mint curls: White hair changes into mint through pointed, angular wedges whose edges coincide with clump construction and shade changes. The three-quarter views expose this as separate painted plates rather than a gradual colour change over rounded locks. Changing only the hair ramp threshold will move the shade boundary without repairing the colour transition.
  - Fix: Retain the continuous base/shade palette mixing in toon.py and revise the authored toon_grad values along each lock's rest arc length. Start with a 10–15 cm smoothstep transition, stagger its start between adjacent locks, and provide at least six longitudinal samples through that interval. In hair_back.clump(), replace the running gmax accumulation with a smooth root-to-tip parameter so the curled return does not inherit a sudden saturated plateau. Keep the attribute attached to the rest mesh during deformation.
- **P1** [naturalness] *back, side, walk front* - exposed upper thighs directly below the petticoat: The skirt assembly projects a scalloped, abrupt shadow edge onto the thighs. It reads as an irregular skin-colour band rather than a simple garment contact shadow, especially from behind; back skin shade coverage is 0.31 versus the sheet's 0.18. The pose sheet makes the uneven boundary conspicuous as the legs separate.
  - Fix: Separate the petticoat's visible ruffles from its broad shadow-casting envelope. Preserve tier-to-tier shade on the visible ruffles, but use a hidden-to-camera, shadow-visible smooth envelope for the thigh-facing shadow silhouette, starting 0.3–0.5 cm inside the ruffle tips. Skin that envelope to the same skirt chains. Implement receiver-selective shadowing where supported; for PMX, use simplified ruffle geometry or a receiver-side material treatment that the target renderer actually supports rather than depending on Blender-only shadow visibility.
- **P1** [naturalness] *side* - skirt side panel immediately below the visible hand: The hand casts a distinct comb of finger shadows onto the pale skirt. Those narrow projections dominate the panel and resemble stains or torn shading, while the intended style describes the cloth with broad masses.
  - Fix: Give the finger geometry a separate shadow-casting policy instead of disabling shadows for the entire body. For the Blender preview, use a simplified palm-and-finger-envelope caster carrying the hand weights, and suppress the individual finger casters while retaining their visible geometry. Keep the proxy inside the hand silhouette and verify the walk and forward-bend poses. If a matching PMX shadow proxy is unavailable, reduce the problem through hand-to-skirt clearance and validate the result in the target MMD renderer.
- **P2** [naturalness] *back, q34_back_right, side* - upper and middle back-hair curtain: Thin grey seams and short pointed shade marks subdivide a largely white curtain, while the side view carries a much broader grey mass. Hair shade coverage changes from 0.52 at the side to 0.25 at the back. Some variation is expected, but the visible distribution emphasizes individual overlapping strips instead of one coherent hair volume.
  - Fix: In hair_back.set_volume_normals(), transfer a continuous envelope normal field across the upper and middle curtain and smoothly blend strand contributions along each lock. Trial reducing VOL_KEEP from 0.30 to approximately 0.15–0.20 on the straight upper lengths while preserving stronger geometric normals on the curls. Blend the cylinder/ellipsoid junction over several rows instead of switching the formula at dz = 0. Tune the hair threshold only after the seam-like patches disappear.
- **P2** [animation] *arms_up front, squat q34_front_left, forward_bend q34_front_left* - sleeve folds, waist-to-skirt transition, and hanging hair: The posed surfaces retain abrupt local shade wedges where the cloth folds and hair change direction. Combined with the zero-width ramp, these narrow regions are likely to switch visibly during motion. The supplied static poses show the spatial problem but do not demonstrate whether it flickers between frames.
  - Fix: After repairing the normal fields, add a per-key ramp-softness override to toon._tune_ramp() and trial a narrow N·L half-width of 0.01–0.02 for hair and pale cloth only. Render short interpolated sequences through arms_up, squat, and head_left under a fixed light to assess patch birth and disappearance. If retained, encode the same narrow transition in export_pmx.toon_ramp(); a Blender-only ramp adjustment will not validate MMD behavior.
- **P2** [fidelity] *front, side, q34_front_left* - green upper-arm ornaments, waist bow, and hanging green ties: Green details read comparatively flat and bright on their front-facing surfaces, with small dark interruptions rather than the sheet's broader dark masses. Front shade coverage is approximately 0.35 versus 0.57 and side coverage is 0.46 versus 0.70; interior colour errors remain high. Treating every green object with one threshold makes it difficult to shade rounded bows and thin ties appropriately.
  - Fix: Allow toon_material() to store an optional material-specific threshold that sync_materials() preserves. Keep the thin ties near the present green threshold, but sweep approximately 0.55–0.65 for the bulky bows and leaf clusters after their normal cleanup. Select settings by coherent shade placement across the turntable, then tune green and leaf palette values independently. Do not force the aggregate shade share by darkening all green pixels.
- **P2** [fidelity] *front, back* - broad skirt panels and white back-hair lengths: The large pale masses have less tonal structure than the sheet even though small shade artifacts remain. Front dress shade coverage is 0.17 versus 0.23, and back hair is 0.25 versus 0.37. The lightened hair and dress shade overrides reduce separation further, so the model can look simultaneously flat overall and noisy locally.
  - Fix: Once the shade shapes are clean, sweep dress thresholds from 0.48 toward 0.52 and hair thresholds from 0.42 toward 0.46 in small increments, rejecting settings that worsen the already heavily shaded side hair. Then compare intermediate shade colours between the current overrides and SHEET_PALETTE, particularly hair #CBD3CD toward #C1C7C1 and dress #D5E2D4 toward #CEDED0. Preserve the fixed reference palette used by the comparison tools.
- **P3** [naturalness] *front, q34_front_left, q34_back_right* - interior skirt hem rolls and overlapping lower hair curls: The narrow doubled contours around the rolled skirt edge and overlapping curls give minor internal borders nearly the same emphasis as the outer silhouette. Keep full width on exposed silhouettes and curl tips. Check both three-quarter views so the reduced hull does not expose gaps.

## skirt

- **P1** [naturalness] *clay front, clay q34_front_left, clay high* - lower skirt fold troughs and rolled hem, especially the front-left group: The lower panels terminate in angular notches with thick, squared-looking returns. The clay views reveal pinched and segmented form at the same locations where the cel render develops mint wedges and broken outlines. This cannot be resolved by softening the material ramp.
  - Fix: Redistribute skirt.T_STEPS to add longitudinal support near the lower folds, for example 0.0, 0.2, 0.4, 0.57, 0.70, 0.80, 0.88, 0.94, 1.0, while keeping within the evaluated face budget. Smooth the crest-to-trough height transition and reduce the local roll radius at pinched troughs rather than applying the full ROLL_R uniformly. Preserve the 0.25 cm physical cloth thickness, and remove sharp normal splits between the main panel and rounded return.
