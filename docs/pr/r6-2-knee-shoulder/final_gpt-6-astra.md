# Codex review: gpt-6-astra

The exposed body reads coherently in the rest views, but the deep squat still produces an over-rounded knee fold, and the raised-arm pose loses shoulder and armpit volume. These are the main body fixes before further silhouette matching: adjust the existing joint helpers and the socket transition geometry rather than adding uniform mesh density. The hands also read as thin, nearly coplanar fingers at oblique angles and would benefit from a stronger palm-to-knuckle transition. The body already has closely spaced joint loops; their presence does not by itself resolve the visible deformation problems. Scores reflect the body-focused review of these still images; wrist twisting, finger curling, hidden hip deformation and exported PMX behavior are not demonstrated by this sheet.

## body

- **P1** [animation] *squat q34_front_left; squat front* - Both knees, especially the outer bend and the compressed hollow immediately behind it: In the deep squat, the exposed knee regions become rounded lobes separated from the shins by tight folds. The three-quarter view makes the loss of a clear kneecap-to-shin transition especially apparent. This is a deformation-volume problem rather than evidence of insufficient overall polygon count: body.py already places closely spaced rings throughout this band.
  - Fix: Keep the existing LEG_ROWS density and revise the knee helper placement and hinge partition against the actual deformed sections. In rig.py, tune JOINT_AUX_CHAIN and JOINT_HINGE together so the front rings wrap around the kneecap while the rear rings compress locally instead of drawing a long section of thigh into the bend. As an initial controlled test, reduce the helper offsets from ±2.5 cm to ±2.0 cm while retaining the current pivot, then compare the 60°, 90° and squat bends before changing KNEE_FWD. Preserve a continuous shin tangent below the lower helper; do not solve the lobe by shrinking the entire knee.
- **P1** [animation] *arms_up front; arms_up q34_front_left* - Both shoulder sockets, along the exposed inner upper arm and anterior armpit fold: Raising the arms leaves narrow, elongated skin bridges between the upper arms and chest, with little rounded shoulder mass. The near shoulder in the three-quarter view reads as a stretched strip rather than a deltoid rolling over an elevated arm. Hair and sleeve bands obscure some of the socket, so the images establish volume loss but do not establish an actual tear.
  - Fix: In body.py arm_rings(), shape the socket transition asymmetrically: retain a fuller outer deltoid while making the inner armpit transition shorter and more compact. Trial an additional 0.3–0.5 cm of fullness in the outer/front socket quadrants, fading to zero at both ends of SOCKET_STEPS. In rig.py, replace the identical all-around SHOULDER_STAGES transition with smoothly varying cap-versus-armpit knots, keeping all stages continuous around the socket. Let the outer cap follow the shoulder stages over a broader distance while the inner fold hands over within a shorter band; verify the result through intermediate arm elevations as well as overhead.
- **P2** [naturalness] *q34_front_left; high; clay q34_front_left* - Both hands, across the palm dome, knuckle line and bases of the four long fingers: The hands read as thin fans of nearly coplanar fingers, with a weak transition from palm to knuckles. The oblique views reveal less palm depth and finger overlap than the frontal silhouette suggests, giving the exposed hands a slightly cutout appearance.
  - Fix: In body.py _palm_shape(), increase the broad dorsal dome by approximately 0.15–0.25 cm and carry it into the knuckle heads with a smooth falloff toward the wrist and palm edges. In FINGERS, stagger the knuckle bases in depth by roughly 0.1–0.2 cm and slightly increase the existing curl progression toward the ring and little fingers. Retain the separate finger components required by the rig, but seat their root rings far enough inside the palm that the added depth does not reveal cylindrical root seams. Check the fist pose after the change.
- **P2** [animation] *walk q34_front_left* - Trailing leg, front and outer contour at the knee-to-upper-shin transition: The moderate knee bend retains a small angular change in direction rather than a continuous anatomical curve. It is less severe than the squat defect, but shows that the knee transition needs to work across intermediate angles as well as at maximum flexion.
  - Fix: Use the walk bend as an additional constraint when revising the squat weighting. In rig.py _split_joint_aux(), inspect the deformed ring tangents where the hinge partition crosses its ±22° knots and where the lower helper hands over to the shin. Broaden only the offending transition over one or two existing LEG_ROWS intervals, approximately 1–2 cm, while preserving the shorter posterior fold. Evaluate the final four-influence weights rather than only the unpruned blend.
