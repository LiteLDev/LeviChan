# Codex review: gpt-6.1-sol

Levi reads consistently around the turntable, but the cel shading still exposes narrow normal discontinuities and angular deformation patches that make pale surfaces look chipped. The most important lookdev fixes are to stabilize hair shading normals and remove the disconnected skirt shade patches introduced by posing; increasing shadow-map filtering will not address those underlying problems. The back-hair tips also combine a mint transition with an abrupt change in shading-normal behavior, making several curls appear capped with a different material. After those issues are resolved, retune the broad shadow coverage on the rear hair, dress, petticoat and green ornaments without chasing aggregate shade percentages at the expense of coherent forms. The supplied poses show no obvious catastrophic tearing, but the squat exposes a clear cloth-shading failure, and the Blender-specific caster policy needs an equivalent export strategy before the PMX appearance can be considered dependable.

Scores: naturalness 6/10, animation 6/10, fidelity 7/10

## hair_back

- **P1** [naturalness] *back, q34_back_right, side* - White-to-mint transitions above the lower hooked curls and on the long outer locks: Several lower locks change from pale hair to a comparatively uniform mint section with an abrupt change in apparent volume. The transition reads as a cap or attached piece rather than pigment fading through one continuous curl. This is intensified by changing the own-normal share as the hair becomes green.
  - Fix: Decouple the normal blend from toon_grad: keep LOW_KEEP from switching shading behavior at the pigment transition and ease toward CURL_KEEP by distance along the curved spine instead. As a starting adjustment, extend G_SPAN from 110 to roughly 140–160 panel pixels while moving G0 upward enough to retain mint at the tips. Reduce SEG_PX from 20 to about 10–12 only through these transitions so colour and normal interpolation have enough samples.

## lookdev

- **P1** [naturalness] *back, q34_back_right, 05_islands back* - Rear hair curtain, especially the narrow strand boundaries from crown to mid-back: The white hair contains many short grey slivers that describe neither broad volume nor deliberate strand accents. There are 55 hair shade islands in back and 41 in q34_back_right; the corresponding clay views also show uneven strand shading. The completely hard ToonRamp exposes these small normal changes as chipped-looking marks.
  - Fix: Keep the two-tone ramp, but regularize its normal input: in hair_back.set_volume_normals(), smooth the own-normal blend spatially along and across each lock, and match neighboring root normals to the shared envelope before the locks separate. Do the equivalent at the front-hair crown overlaps. Compare identical renders with and without cast shadows to isolate the remaining caster contribution; do not increase SHADOW_FILTER as the primary fix.
- **P1** [animation] *squat q34_front_left, squat front, walk q34_front_left* - Outer skirt front, between the waist bow and the middle of the hem: Posing produces isolated triangular and polygonal mint shade patches on otherwise continuous pale cloth. They are most apparent in the squat and smaller in the walk, so this is a deformation-dependent shading defect rather than just a rest-pose palette mismatch. The hard ramp makes local dents or normal changes read as separate panels.
  - Fix: Inspect the evaluated skirt with the toon_grad contribution temporarily held constant to distinguish geometry shading from interpolated colour. Smooth skirt-chain weight derivatives across adjacent angular sectors and height bands, add an intermediate T_STEPS row where the squat bend is concentrated, and preserve smooth normals across all interior cloth edges. If the deformed surface still has small normal ripples, transfer shading normals from a smoothly deformed low-frequency skirt envelope; retain the real fold normals near the hem.
- **P2** [naturalness] *high, q34_back_right* - Exposed upper thighs immediately below the petticoat: The thigh contact shadows terminate in conspicuously straight, abrupt bands. In the high view, each leg looks divided into rectangular colour zones, which weakens the cylindrical leg volume even though the total shaded skin area is close to the reference.
  - Fix: Refine render.build_hem_proxy() so its lower cap follows the smoothed hem profile rather than joining every sector to a single mean-height center. Tune the skin proxy lift around the current 4 cm in 0.5 cm increments, checking high and three-quarter views as well as front. Give only this contact-shadow transition a narrow feather, keeping the broad skin terminator crisp.
- **P2** [naturalness] *q34_back_right, side, back* - Pink petticoat underside, particularly the exposed rear-right folds: The petticoat becomes a broad, comparatively dark pink shelf with abrupt vertical shade divisions around protruding folds. Its shade share reaches 0.64 at q34_back_right and 0.57 in back, making the layered fabric read heavier and more slab-like than the outer dress.
  - Fix: Separate petticoat self-shading from the skirt contact shadow when tuning: compare the existing caster policy with a version where the petticoat does not shadow itself. Add a petticoat-specific RAMP_BY_KEY entry and test approximately 0.20–0.25 against the current default 0.30, preserving a narrow contact band from +skirt_hem. Correct local fold normals where dark divisions persist instead of globally lightening both pink tones.
- **P2** [animation] *04_poses, export_pmx.py* - Shadow relationships among the skirt, petticoat, thighs and hanging ornaments: The reviewed appearance relies on EEVEE receiver-specific caster groups and invisible hem proxies. The export code enables PMX self-shadow flags broadly, so that selective shadow behavior is not represented by the exported material settings. The supplied images therefore do not establish that the cleaned-up cloth and thigh shadows will survive in MMD.
  - Fix: Add an explicit PMX shadow policy in export_pmx.py instead of enabling enabled_self_shadow_map and enabled_self_shadow for every material. Disable casting for the same thin ornament materials excluded in Blender, then choose and document a supported compromise for cloth and skin receivers. Render the exported walk and squat in the intended MMD renderer; where receiver-specific control is unavailable, use portable broad contact tinting and fewer real casters rather than relying on the EEVEE proxies.
- **P2** [fidelity] *back, q34_back_right* - Broad white rear-hair mass from shoulder height to the curl roots: The rear hair is dominated by one flat pale field with thin strand slivers, whereas the reference organizes it into broad shaded locks. Back hair shade coverage is 0.19 versus 0.37 in the sheet, and the tuned hair shade is also lighter than the measured sheet shade. Simply raising the hair threshold would risk increasing the existing slivers.
  - Fix: After cleaning the normal field, sweep hair RAMP_BY_KEY from 0.42 toward 0.46–0.48 and move the hair shade from #CBD3CD partway toward the sheet's #C1C7C1. Select the result using broad connected shade shapes in back and q34_back_right, while ensuring the front hair does not become a collection of dark strand stripes.
- **P2** [fidelity] *front, side, high* - Dark green upper-arm bows, leaf ornaments and their overlapping folds: The green details are comparatively flat and bright within each form, losing the overlapping folded structure visible in the sheet. Green shade coverage is about 0.33 versus 0.57 in front and 0.37 versus 0.70 in side; interior colour errors remain high, so this is not explained solely by thin-part alignment.
  - Fix: Tune green and leaf independently in toon.RAMP_BY_KEY: test green around 0.55–0.60 while retaining a lower leaf threshold so leaves remain distinct from ribbons. Remove isolated normal spikes on bow folds before increasing coverage, and compare base/shade swatches separately rather than darkening the entire green region to match its mean.
- **P2** [fidelity] *back, q34_front_left, high* - Outer dress below the rear hair and across the broad skirt folds: The rear dress is too uniformly pale, while the visible side shade often resolves into one long straight wedge. The reference uses several broad curved fold accents; back dress shade coverage is 0.17 versus 0.30. The current distribution emphasizes the skirt's shell shape more than its fabric folds.
  - Fix: Separate bodice and skirt ramp control through a material-level threshold override in toon.py, preserving their shared palette. Test a modest increase from 0.48 to approximately 0.51–0.53 for the skirt only after the pose patches are fixed. In skirt.py, use the existing drifting fold coordinates for broad, feathered accents instead of increasing the global STREAK amount, which would also strengthen the straight wedges.
- **P3** [fidelity] *back, q34_back_right, front* - Internal hair overlaps and pale garment fold boundaries: Internal hair definition alternates between nearly invisible pale edges and numerous fine scratch-like lines, while some dress boundaries are comparatively uniform. This gives the render less deliberate line hierarchy than the painted sheet and makes remaining shade slivers more visually prominent.
  - Fix: Use add_outline() vertex-group thickness control consistently: keep the outer silhouette near the current 1.25 panel pixels and reduce buried internal overlaps to roughly 0.5–0.7 of that width. Preserve a few continuous major lock boundaries rather than outlining every buried strand equally. Tune the hair and dress line colours independently after the shade-island cleanup so stronger lines do not disguise unresolved shading noise.
